Psychological Safety Check

Leader view

Free · About two minutes · Built on Amy Edmondson's research

You think your team tells you everything.Do they?

Leaders almost always believe their team is safer to speak up in than the team does. Take the leader view of a published psychological safety scale, then find out how close your guess is.

Take it free How it works with a whole team

  • Seven statements
  • No fee
  • Team answers stay anonymous

Why it matters

The person at the top is the last to hear

  • 85%

    of employees interviewed in one study had, at least once, stayed silent with their boss about an issue they thought was important.

    Milliken, Morrison and Hewlin, Journal of Management Studies, 2003 (40 interviews)
  • 1,440

    clinicians in 23 intensive care units. Higher-status professionals felt significantly safer than the people they worked with, and inclusive leaders narrowed that gap.

    Nembhard and Edmondson, Journal of Organizational Behavior, 2006

How it works

Your guess, their answers, and the gap between

  1. You take the leader view

    Rate seven statements the way you think your team would. It takes about two minutes and costs nothing.

  2. Your team answers

    The same seven statements, anonymously, with a team code instead of names. Results only once at least five people respond.

  3. We look at the gap

    Thirty minutes on where your guess and their answers part ways, and the first thing worth redesigning.
