Building the Bench

Emerging leaders

How many deep are you at each position?

The question is not whether your leaders are any good. It is what happens the week one of them resigns. Building the Bench is a cohort for the people who could step into the next seat.

Book thirty minutes For companies or for schools

What is in it

The cohort is the point

Growing scope, real consequence, and the problems that never appear in a training module because they are about other adults.

  • Leaders at the same altitudeFrom different parts of the organization, facing the same next step.
  • Real problems, out loudThe only room most new leaders have to describe a real problem to people who understand the job.
  • A first call for yearsThese people become each other’s first call long after the program ends.
  • Cohorts from five participantsIn person or hybrid. Priced per cohort, on a call.

Why build it inside

The person already in the building is usually the better answer

Wharton research on six years of personnel data found external hires are paid 18 to 20% more than internal promotions, review significantly worse for their first two years, and leave sooner. Developing the bench is the cheaper answer and usually the better one.

  1. Scheduling

    Around the work

    Evenings, professional development days, or whatever gaps the calendar actually has. The people who most need this are the hardest to release during the day.

  2. Format

    In person or hybrid

    Five to ten participants, so every case gets worked properly.

  3. Length

    Six months, or a school year

    Paced to how your organization plans and budgets.
