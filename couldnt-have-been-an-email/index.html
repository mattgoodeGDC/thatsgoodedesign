This couldn't have been an email?

Couldn't Have Been an Email is a free guide to meeting rhythms for schools – and the follow-through that makes the time count. A companion to Field Tested.

Matt Goode

Run the meeting that couldn't have been an email; send the email that shouldn't have been a meeting.

    Free · 25 pages · PDF and EPUB, plus editable forms. It arrives in your inbox in a minute or two.

    About the Companion

    Every minute in a meeting is a minute not spent planning, grading, or catching a student who needs you. This companion is about spending that time well: the meetings a school actually runs, each on one page, with a clock, an owner for every decision, and a way to close the loop.

    A meeting is for the work only a room can do. If it could be an email, make it one.

    It was built for schools and works for anyone who runs a meeting. Every rhythm has a suggested length and a window, a structure you can follow to the minute, opening questions, and the Field Tested methods that pair with it. The research behind it is cited, and the facilitation advice is written for rooms full of people who redirect conversations for a living.

    What's inside

    • 1
      The case for fewer, better meetings.What the research says, and why psychological safety matters more than any timer.
    • 2
      Ten meeting rhythms.Weekly staff, monthly team, grade-team operations, faculty, department, topics of concern, one-on-ones, after action reviews, students of concern and student support teams.
    • 3
      Running the room.Moves for keeping skilled talkers on track without scolding, and the 6C Decision Compass for naming how a call gets made.
    • 4
      Designed for every mind.Thinking time, more than one way in, and room setups matched to the work.
    • 5
      A Students of Concern system.The meeting, a follow-up lifecycle so no student sits on the list untouched, and a referral form and tracker you can edit in Word, Excel or Google.

    For a teacher, the math is brutal and simple. Subtract the hours spent live in front of students, and everything left in the day is zero-sum.

    It's the guide I wish I'd had, on both sides of the table: as the facilitator trying to make an hour count, and as the teacher giving up a lunch break to be there. I hope it helps.
