Why this is different

You are not buying a methodology with a team attached

A firm sells you a partner and staffs the work with people you have not met. These engagements are the other shape. Five differences, all structural, all checkable.

  • The same person, start to finish. Whoever reads your diagnostic is whoever is in the room a year later. There is no associate layer and no handoff after the sale, which is the whole reason the second half of an engagement is usually better than the first.
  • It starts from instruments, not interviews. CliftonStrengths® and EQ-i 2.0, read against each other rather than reported side by side. Most engagements open with a fortnight of listening and a hypothesis. This one opens with data about how the people in the room actually operate under pressure.
  • COACHING and CONSULTING in one engagement, named. A coach will not tell you what to do. A consultant will not stop telling you. Both modes are in scope here, you always know which one is running, and you can ask to switch mid-conversation.
  • The individual work stays private even when the company pays. Sponsors hear that an engagement is active and nothing else without written consent. That is not a courtesy; it is the condition that makes the one-to-one work honest enough to be worth buying.
  • Scope gets renegotiated at the midpoint, by me. Halfway through, I open the agreement and ask what has changed. A firm has no incentive to raise that question and every incentive to deliver what was sold.

How this fits CLUTCH

Emotional intelligence is the only part engineered to slip

CLUTCH treats an organization as a drivetrain. Every other component is built to grip. The clutch is built to slip, on purpose, so that parts turning at different speeds can engage without shearing. In a leader, that mechanism is emotional intelligence, and it is what connects CREATE, VOICES, the 6C Compass and DELEGATE. Strengths tell you what your engine is built to do. EQ tells you how well it engages with everyone else's. The OBD reads both, because a strong engine with a slipping clutch still goes nowhere.
