Sample · fictional leader

OBD read: Dana R.

VP Engineering, 60-person software company. Fourteen months in the role.

1Leadership snapshot

Under pressure, Dana narrows. Both reports point the same way: she is at her best working a hard problem herself, quickly and thoroughly, and her read of other people drops furthest exactly when deadlines tighten. Her team experiences this as silence. Decisions arrive finished.

2Your strengths pattern

Her strengths cluster around getting hard things done and getting them right. Very little in her profile pulls toward persuading a room. When Dana changes someone's mind, she does it with evidence, not energy, and only once she is sure.

3What is running under the hood

  • Reading other people●●●●○
  • Saying it out loud, early●●○○○
  • Holding steady under pressure●●●○○
  • Deciding without waiting●●●○○

She notices more than she says. The gap between the two is the widest reading in her profile.

4Where your strengths and EQ meet

Her strengths say she earns trust through competence. Her EQ results say she rarely lets people see how she is doing. So her team trusts her work and guesses at her mood, and the guessing gets louder the closer a deadline is.

5Your engine: the six CREATE cylinders

  • ExecutionStrongest cylinder. Carries the team through every release.
  • RelationshipsRuns lean under load. Pays for Execution when the pace rises.
  • TeachingStrong one to one. Not yet built into how the team works.

6Your transmission: the six VOICES gears

Operator is her default gear, and a good one. Independent is the gear she reaches for least: when she disagrees with a decision from above, she executes it well and says nothing. Her team reads the silence as agreement.

7How you show up

Dana is the person everyone wants on the hard problem, and the last person anyone tells about a hard week.

8Your highest-leverage work

  1. Make one decision a week out loud, before it is finished, with the people who will carry it out in the room.
  2. Name one disagreement upward each month, in the meeting, before the decision closes.
  3. Hand one recurring fix to a direct report, and do not take it back.

What to watch at ninety days: whether her direct reports start bringing her problems before they have solved them, or after.

9Questions for the road ahead

  • What does your team know about where you stand because you told them, and what have they only inferred?
  • Which problem did you solve yourself this month that someone else should have owned?

Why this is different

You are not buying a methodology with a team attached

A firm sells you a partner and staffs the work with people you have not met. These engagements are the other shape. Five differences, all structural, all checkable.

  • The same person, start to finish. Whoever reads your diagnostic is whoever is in the room a year later. There is no associate layer and no handoff after the sale, which is the whole reason the second half of an engagement is usually better than the first.
  • It starts from instruments, not interviews. CliftonStrengths® and EQ-i 2.0, read against each other rather than reported side by side. Most engagements open with a fortnight of listening and a hypothesis. This one opens with data about how the people in the room actually operate under pressure.
  • COACHING and CONSULTING in one engagement, named. A coach will not tell you what to do. A consultant will not stop telling you. Both modes are in scope here, you always know which one is running, and you can ask to switch mid-conversation.
  • The individual work stays private even when the company pays. Sponsors hear that an engagement is active and nothing else without written consent. That is not a courtesy; it is the condition that makes the one-to-one work honest enough to be worth buying.
  • Scope gets renegotiated at the midpoint, by me. Halfway through, I open the agreement and ask what has changed. A firm has no incentive to raise that question and every incentive to deliver what was sold.

How this fits CLUTCH

Emotional intelligence is the only part engineered to slip

CLUTCH treats an organization as a drivetrain. Every other component is built to grip. The clutch is built to slip, on purpose, so that parts turning at different speeds can engage without shearing. In a leader, that mechanism is emotional intelligence, and it is what connects CREATE, VOICES, the 6C Compass and DELEGATE.

Strengths tell you where your power comes from. EQ tells you whether that power reaches anyone else. The OBD reads both, because power that never engages goes nowhere.

    Yes. The read is designed to be useful on its own and portable to any coach or supervisor.
