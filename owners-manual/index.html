Owner’s Manual

Team workshop · built on the OBD

Write the manual for how you work. Then swap.

A three-hour workshop for a small team that works together every day. Each person turns their OBD into an Owner’s Manual: how they lead, what they need from colleagues, where their edges are. Then the team swaps, so nobody has to guess how anyone else works.

Book thirty minutes For companies or for schools

What is in it

  • A manual for each personWritten from their own OBD data, not a quiz on the day, so it holds up afterward.
  • The swapManuals are traded across the team, which is the part that changes how it works on Monday.
  • Built on the OBDEvery participant needs an OBD, invoiced separately. The manual can’t be written without it.
  • Up to 15 peopleFor an admin, executive or product team that works together daily. Three hours on its own, or a full day with Know Thyself to Lead Others.

Individual diagnostics, made legible to the whole team

From one OBD to a team that works

  1. 01 / Diagnose

    The OBD read

    Each person’s CliftonStrengths® and EQ-i results, read together through CREATE and VOICES.

  2. 02 / Translate

    The Owner’s Manual

    Each person writes one page: at my best, what I need, what you can expect, my flex gear, my basement tell.

  3. 03 / Read together

    The swap

    The manuals are traded, and the pattern everyone has been managing around gets named out loud.

  4. 04 / Agree

    Working agreements

    Up to five, written against real data instead of good intentions, and checked at day 60.

Run across a team, the manuals show the pattern it has been managing around silently: three Operators and no Visionary, a Command default in a room that needs Consultative, four people waiting on the same person to decide. Naming it turns private friction into a design problem the team can solve.

One page, five sections

How to work with me, on one page

  1. 1

    At my best

    When I’m at my best, I ___, so you get ___.

    “When I’m at my best, I map the path through the mess, so you get a next step, not just a meeting.”

  2. 2

    What I need

    I do my best work when ___. Help me by ___.

    “I do my best work with time to think. Send me the question the day before.”

  3. 3

    What you can expect

    Even on a bad week, you can count on me to ___.

    “Even on a bad week, I’ll tell you whether a decision is open or already made.”

  4. 4

    My flex gear

    The gear I’m growing into is ___. It costs me ___, and ___ has been waiting on it.

    “Operator. Closing loops costs me, so I run on lists, and my project leads have been waiting for decisions on Fridays.”

  5. 5

    My basement tell

    Under pressure, my ___ turns into ___. What helps is ___.

    “Under pressure I go quiet. Quiet means I’m thinking, not upset. Ask what I’m chewing on.”

Every line has to pass three tests before it goes in.

SpecificIt could only be about you.
UsableIt tells someone what to do on Monday.
HonestIt includes a cost or a bad day.

What the team leaves with: a one-page manual each, a three-line version for a profile or email signature, a shared “How we work” page that new joiners read in their first week, and up to five working agreements written against real data rather than good intentions.

What a finished manual looks like

One page, five sections, each line tested. Every section notes where the line came from in the writer’s OBD, so the page is built on evidence, not on a good mood.

The sample is Sam, the fictional leader from the workshop’s worked example. Yours is written by you, in your words, and you decide who sees it.

How it runs

Two weeks before

Ready before the room

  • Every OBD done. No OBD, no manual.
  • A baseline. The team takes the Psychological Safety Check, so day 60 has something to compare against.
  • The lead goes first. On a thirty-minute call, the lead writes their manual, bad day included, and it goes out to the team.
  • Twenty minutes of pre-work. Read your OBD summary, and ask one colleague two questions: what do I look like under pressure, and what’s the best way to give me feedback?

Three hours

Write it, then swap it

  • Harvest. Mark what your OBD gets right, what surprises you and what you push back on, then set it against your colleague’s view.
  • Gears. Your home gear and flex gear on the six VOICES modes, and your first move on the 6C Decision Compass.
  • Write. The five sections, one at a time, each line tested.
  • Swap. Pairs read each other’s manuals, say one line back in their own words, and ask questions. No advice.
  • Team. The team gearbox, and up to five agreements: “When ___, we will ___.”

Sixty days after

Make it hold

  • Within two days, the team’s “How we work” page: agreements, gearbox and an owner.
  • Weeks two and three, fifteen minutes in a team meeting: one line back from a colleague’s reply, lead first.
  • Day 60, everyone marks each manual line “true”, “partly” or “not yet”, the Safety Check runs again, and we rewrite what didn’t hold.
  • The sponsor gets a one-page summary: which agreements held and what moved. Never anyone’s manual.

The rules in the room

  • You choose every line.Nothing from your OBD reaches the room unless you write it in yourself, and you decide whether your manual goes in the team folder.
  • The lead goes first.People write as honestly as the most senior person in the room, so the lead reads their basement tell aloud in the first ten minutes.
  • Curiosity, not advice.In the swap, readers ask questions. Nobody fixes anyone.
  • Say it, then write it.Some of us think better out loud. Talk an answer through with a partner or into your phone, and write it down after.
