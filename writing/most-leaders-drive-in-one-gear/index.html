Most Leaders Drive in One Gear

Matt Goode20 June 20263 min read Get new pieces by email

# VOICES

I know my default.

I know my default. Put me under pressure and I reach for Sculptor. Most leaders aim that gear at the people around them, sharpening everyone else's work until nothing leaves the room unpolished. I aimed it at myself. I sharpened my own work long past the point anyone needed it sharp, and called the delay a standard. It's the gear that made the work good, which is exactly why it took me years to see it as the thing keeping the work from ever being done.

Every leader has one of these. The gear you grab when the heat rises, the one that feels like leadership because it’s the one that got you here.

Six gears, one habit

VOICES is the gearbox: Visionary, Operator, Independent, Coach, Encourager, Sculptor. Six modes of presence, one for each kind of moment. Visionary zooms out and reframes. Operator gets into the work. Independent steps back so others carry the weight. Coach builds judgment through questions instead of answers. Encourager fuels belief. Sculptor sharpens the work until it’s sharp.

Caption: VOICES – six gears, one for each kind of moment.

The skill was never picking a favorite. It’s reading the grade and shifting before you redline. Stay in first on the open highway, and you smother a team with control they’ve outgrown. Drop into fifth on a steep climb, and you’re casting vision at people who needed you in the room, deciding one hard thing.

The trap is that under stress, the whole gearbox collapses into your default. The founder who built on a vision keeps casting vision to a team that needs a decision. The operator keeps diving into tasks when the team needs to be grown rather than directed. The gear isn’t the problem. The reflex is.

What does the stuck gear cost you

A team that waits. Stay in Operator, and people learn to bring you the problem instead of the solution, because you’ll take it anyway. You feel busy and indispensable. They feel small and a little bored. Boredom in a capable person isn’t harmless; it’s a countdown. Two years of that, and your best engineer is taking a recruiter’s call, looking for a job where they actually get to drive.

There’s a number under this, too. Gallup’s research keeps landing on the same finding: clear roles and real development drive engagement by about 20 to 30 percent, and engaged teams simply produce more, churn less, and cost less to keep. The leader stuck in one gear is leaving that on the table every week, and calling it dedication.

The single most useful shift for most leaders is the downshift into Coach. The urge to grab the wheel and answer is usually the exact moment someone in front of you was ready to find the answer themselves. It’s slower this week. It’s the only thing that pays off the week you’re out.

And the cost lands first on the people you can least afford to lose. Your most capable person has the most options and the least patience for being managed in a gear they outgrew two promotions ago. The leader who can’t shift out of their default doesn’t lose their weakest performer to it. They lose their strongest.

Catch yourself this week, then sit with these:

  • What gear do you reach for in the first ten seconds of a hard moment?

  • Who in front of you needed Coach this week and got your default instead?

  • Where might your strongest gear be the very thing capping the people you lead?
