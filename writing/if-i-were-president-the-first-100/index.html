If I Were President: The First 100 Days

MALAA - Make America Less Annoying Again

Matt Goode30 December 202510 min read Get new pieces by email

# Off the clock

During the 2020 presidential election, I was teaching middle school. Like many teachers, I wanted my students to understand the political process without turning the classroom into a cable-news reenactment. So instead of debating candidates, we studied how platforms are built – what they include, what they signal, and what they reveal about values.

One of their projects was simple: research a few issues, create a party platform, and deliver a stump speech.

Yes, an actual stump. I brought one into the classroom.

The assignment was loosely inspired by NPR’s This I Believe series – not because I wanted polished policy proposals, but because I wanted clarity. What do you believe? What frustrates you? What would you fix first if you had the power?

What surprised me wasn’t the politics. It was the patterns.

Across wildly different viewpoints, the same complaints kept surfacing: systems that wasted time, rules that made no sense, processes that felt intentionally confusing. The kids weren’t arguing left versus right. They were arguing why things were so needlessly hard.

This piece is a grown-up version of that assignment.

It’s not satire, exactly. And it’s not a serious campaign platform, either. It’s a thought experiment built around a simple premise: before we ask people to trust institutions with the hardest problems, maybe we should prove we can fix the small, obvious ones.

What follows is my imaginary first hundred days as president – focused not on ideology, but on friction. On the little design failures that quietly drain time, money, and patience from everyday life.

Consider it a stump speech for common sense.

====

My fellow Americans, we’re more divided than ever. But you know what unites us? We ALL hate changing our clocks twice a year. We ALL lose the TV remote in the couch. We ALL wonder why avocados are either rocks or mush with no in-between.

Here’s my platform: Fix the stuff that makes everyone’s life unnecessarily complicated.

No red states. No blue states. Just people who want their grocery store to have milk in the same place every time.

This isn’t about ideology. It’s about eliminating stupid friction so Americans can focus on what actually matters. Three problems everyone hates. Three solutions everyone understands. Zero committees harmed.

Executive Order #1:

End Daylight Saving Time

Arizona and Hawaii were right all along.

Executive Order #2:

Move the Super Bowl to Saturday

If the host city is on the West Coast, the kickoff is 1:00 pm local time. The biggest party of the year shouldn’t require a sick day.

Executive Order #3:

Halloween Is Always the Last Friday in October

Consistent. Safe. Parents can enjoy it. Kids get maximum candy. Costume parties don’t collide with school nights. Done.

“If government can’t fix the obvious stuff that makes daily life harder than it needs to be, it has no business tackling the complicated stuff.”

Week 1: Democracy, Just A Little Less Sucky

So let me be explicit. In the opening phase of this administration, I am not issuing executive orders or fast-tracking legislation on:

  • Campaign finance reform

  • Open or closed primaries

  • Ranked-choice voting

  • Electoral college reform

  • Constitutional amendments of any kind

Not because they’re off the table forever – but because they don’t belong in the opening move. I have very serious thoughts about all of those issues and how to reform and remake our great Republic. But, this isn’t that kind of post. Those issues require: Broad public deliberation, Legislative buy-in, clear sequencing, and, most importantly, trust that the process itself isn’t rigged.

Double the Size of the House of Representatives: Sidestepping the potential for more gerrymandering shithousery, and the Constitutional considerations of an Executive messing with Article 1, just play along for this first one. 435 representatives for 335 million people means each rep has 770,000 constituents. That’s too many. Double it to 870 reps. Smaller districts = better representation = government closer to the people. Fight me on that.

Automatic Voter Registration: You turn 18, you’re registered. Opt-out if you want. Democracy should be opt-out, not opt-in.

Week 2: My Body is Not Your Profit Center

End Pharmaceutical Ads on TV: You don’t need a commercial telling you to “ask your doctor about” a drug you can’t pronounce for a condition you never heard of. If you need medicine, your doctor will tell you. Every other developed country figured this out, except, strangely, New Zealand.

Taxpayer Dividend on Drugs: If a pharmaceutical drug was developed using any federally funded research, it must be sold to U.S. citizens at cost + 10%. You shouldn’t pay for the “innovation” twice – once in taxes and again at the pharmacy. If a company refuses, the patent becomes public property immediately.

If Doctors Charge for Missed Appointments, Patients Can Charge for Delays: Your doctor charges $100 if you’re a no-show? Fine. But if your appointment starts more than 15 minutes late, your fee drops by $10 for each 10-minute delay. Waiting room time is YOUR time too.

Medical Necessity Means Medical Necessity: Insurance may challenge fraud. Insurance may require second opinions. Insurance may not override clinical judgment with a spreadsheet or AI. Doctors practice medicine. Insurance companies process claims. Those roles should not be interchangeable.

Plain-English Medical Bills: One page. Total cost. What insurance paid. What you owe. No more decoding 14-page statements written in medical billing hieroglyphics.

Week 3: If You’re the Product - You Get Paid

Opt-In Data Sales Only: Companies cannot sell or share customer data unless you explicitly opt in. No pre-checked boxes. No “by using this site…” nonsense. Your data belongs to you.

The Data Dividend: If a company sells your anonymized data to a third party, you are entitled to a “Data Royalty.” A percentage of that sale must be credited back to your account or sent to a national digital trust fund in your name. If you are the product, you should get paid.

Week 4: I Customer, You Service

Subscription Cancellation Fairness: If you can sign up in two clicks, you can cancel in two clicks. No phone calls. No retention specialists. No “tell us why you’re leaving.” If a company can take your money instantly, it must let you stop just as easily.

Junk Mail Is Illegal: Mail delivered to “Current Resident,” “Occupant,” or any name that is not yours is prohibited. If someone wants to reach you, they should know who you are.

Stop Asking Me About My Car’s Warranty: Robocalls referencing expired vehicle warranties, or that I am prequalified for a loan, or urgent legal matters exist to scare and deceive. Any call in this genre will be blocked at the carrier level and subject to a per-attempt fine. If a message is legitimate, it can be sent in writing.

Press Zero for a Human: At any point in an automated phone call, pressing “0” must connect you to a live human being. No endless menus. No voice recognition games. No looping into oblivion. If a company is large enough to use an automated phone system, it is large enough to provide a person who can help. Pressing zero means I need a person. And the answer should always be yes.

Week 5: Technology Works For You Now

Video Calls Work Like Text Messages: Zoom, Teams, FaceTime, Google Meet – full interoperability required. Just like you can text between an iPhone and an Android, you should be able to video call across platforms. The technology exists. Make it happen.

One Charger to Rule Them All: USB-C for everything. iPhone, Android, laptops, tablets, and electric toothbrushes. Companies have 18 months to comply or pay a “proprietary connector tax.” Ditto: a subset for lawn and power tools, etc., and yet another for EVs. Plugs are standardized on one end; why not on both?

Social Media Chronological Feed Mandate: Every platform must offer a “show me everything chronologically” option. No algorithm required. Just posts from people I follow, in order. Let ME decide what I want to see. Gosh, remember Tweetdeck, iykyk.

TV Remote “Find Me” Button: Every TV sold in America must have a “find my remote” Bluetooth button that makes the remote beep. This solves a problem in 100 million households instantly.

Internet Forms – Stop Asking for State AND Zip Code: Your zip code contains your state. Databases exist. If I enter 21042, the system knows I’m in Maryland. Stop making me type both.

The “Low Battery” Warning Standard: Smoke detectors and smart devices are prohibited from chirping “Low Battery” between the hours of 10:00 PM and 8:00 AM. They must wait until a civilized hour to demand a 9-volt battery change.

Week 6: Make Flying Suck Less

The Middle Seat Gets Both Armrests: It’s not left or right politics – it’s basic human decency. You’re squished between two strangers at 30,000 feet. You get the armrests.

TSA PreCheck: $10, Lasts Forever: Security theater shouldn’t be a profit center. Background check costs $10. You pay once. You’re good forever unless you commit a crime. Simple.

Plane Tarmac Rule Enhancement: If the plane sits on the tarmac for 30+ minutes, everyone gets free WiFi for the entire flight. Airlines will suddenly figure out how to manage delays better.

Week 7: Stop Making Driving Harder

Gas Pump Simplification Act: Insert card. Pump gas. Done. No zip code. No car wash offer. No receipt question. No rewards program pitch. No solicitation for X charity – you’re a multibillion-dollar petro conglomerate, YOU round up and donate.

Zipper Merge is Mandatory: Taught in every driver’s ed course. Signs on every highway. Merge at the merge point, not at the very end. Traffic flow improves. Road rage decreases. This is infrastructure policy and will be a significant focus of the Department of Transportation.

Construction Zone Transparency: Every construction zone must display what’s happening and when it ends. Areas with cones and without workers lose “construction zone penalties” after two hours.

The “Actual Safety” Speed Camera Law: Speed cameras can only be placed in high-accident zones or school zones – never at the bottom of a hill or behind a hidden sign. 100% of the fine must go toward local road repairs or the nearby school, not the general city fund or a private camera company.

Week 8: Grocery Shopping, But A Little Less Annoying

Standardized Grocery Store Layouts: I don’t care what the actual layout is, but every grocery store in the country must follow the same basic template. You should be able to walk into any grocery store in America and know where the milk is. This saves millions of collective hours annually.

Bananas, Melons, and Avocados Sorted by Ripeness: Stores must sort this genre of produce into three bins: “Today,” “2-3 Days,” “4-5 Days.” Science and supply chain coordination make this possible.

Self-Checkout is 5% Cheaper: You’re doing the work. You get the discount. Also, those weight sensors are abolished – just scan and go.

Week 9: Let’s Live Like Neighbors Again

Government-Funded “Library of Things”: Every county has a Library of Things where you can check out a power drill, a ladder, a pressure washer, and a carpet cleaner for free. Nobody needs to own a tool they use once a year. Saves Americans billions. Reduces waste. Builds community.

No Gas-Powered Leaf Blowers: Gas leaf blowers are loud, polluting, and unnecessary. Get a rake. Use an electric blower if you must. Or be happy with a composting lawn. Leaves are not trash – they’re mulch. Your neighbors, your kids, and your lungs will all be better off.

Week 10: School Is Hard Enough As Is

School Start Times for Teens - 9 am or Later: The science is clear. Teenagers’ brains aren’t awake at 7 am. Later start times improve grades and mental health and reduce car accidents. This is a no-brainer.

No Homework Unless It’s Reading or Practicing an Instrument: Elementary school kids don’t need worksheets. They need to play, read, and sleep. If homework doesn’t involve reading books or music practice, it doesn’t go home.

School Communications Come in One Weekly Digest: Each school sends one predictable, once-a-week communication with everything parents need to know: events, deadlines, forms, schedule changes. Emergencies are the only exception.

Week 11: Suck It HR

All Job Posts Must Include Salary and Benefits: Upfront. In the posting. No more “competitive salary” or “depends on experience.” Salary range required. Health insurance details required. PTO policy required.

Job Descriptions Written by the Actual Manager: Not HR. Not a recruiter. The person who will manage the role writes the description. If they can’t articulate what they need, the position doesn’t exist.

Interview Process Fairness Rules: More than 3 rounds? Candidates get paid $100/hour for each additional round. “Fast-paced environment” must be defined in plain English. If I upload a resume, auto-fill the application – don’t make me type my work history twice.

Week 12: Shower Time

Shampoo Bottles That Don’t All Look the Same: For the 100% of Americans over 50 who wear readers, shampoo, conditioner, and body wash bottles must have different shapes or raised text you can read by touch. Taking a shower shouldn’t require perfect vision.

Standardized Shower Temperature Controls: Every shower has a “favorite temperature” button. My car is coded to adjust the seats and mirrors to my fob, rather than my wife’s settings. Set it once, press it every time. No more scalding or freezing while you find the sweet spot.

THE CAMPAIGN SLOGAN

“Let’s fix the annoying stuff first. Then we’ll tackle the hard problems.”
