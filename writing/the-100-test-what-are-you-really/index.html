The $100 Test: What Are You Really Leading With?

Matt Goode1 October 20252 min read Get new pieces by email

# CREATE

Your calendar doesn’t lie. Neither does your energy.

Leadership isn’t just about what you say you value – it’s about where you actually invest.

Years ago, during a strategic retreat with a USAID team, we skipped the spreadsheets and handed out stacks of Monopoly money. Their mission? Build a budget for the next year – knowing they had “mortgages” (aka fixed commitments) they couldn’t change.

The money was fake, but the clarity was real.

Seeing teams physically allocate their budget forced hard truths:

  • What do we always say is important… but never really fund?

  • What’s getting money just because it always has?

  • What’s starving that we claim to care about?

This wasn’t about budget planning. It was about priority alignment.
About matching behavior to belief.

And it works just as well with leadership.

This Week’s Coaching Prompt:
“If I had $100 to spend on leadership this week – where would I invest it?”

Here’s one way to break it down:

  • $30 → Building trust

  • $25 → Clarifying goals

  • $20 → Giving and receiving feedback

  • $15 → Cultivating your own clarity and calm

  • $10 → Delegating what only you think you should own

Try this reflection:

  • Where am I overspending – out of habit or fear?

  • What am I underfunding – despite knowing it matters?

  • What’s one “mortgage” I’ve accepted that needs rethinking?

Why This Matters Right Now
We’re at the end of the fiscal year. Budgets are being closed out, but leadership costs carry over.

Burnout. Misalignment. Low trust. Constant firefighting.
They all stem from misallocated energy.

The $100 metaphor is simple – but sticky.
You can use it in your own reflection. In team retreats. Even in 1:1s.

Because saying “team culture is everything” and then investing $0 in psychological safety?

That gap is the work.

Your Move: Run a $100 Audit This Week

  1. Set a timer for 10 minutes.

  2. Allocate your $100 honestly across your leadership investments.

  3. Ask: Does this match what I say matters?

  4. Share it with a peer – or your team.

Bonus: Pull out some Monopoly money. Make it real. You’d be surprised at how quickly clarity emerges when you make priorities tangible. Order it on Amazon, here is what I bought.

Share this with a colleague navigating FY planning – or a leader who wants their choices to match their values.

You don’t need a bigger budget.
You need a clearer allocation.

So:
Where’s your $100 going?
