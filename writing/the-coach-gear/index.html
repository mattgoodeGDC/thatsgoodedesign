The Coach Gear

Matt Goode19 July 20262 min read Get new pieces by email

# VOICES# Coaching

Someone on your team brings you a problem. You see the answer immediately – you’ve solved this exact thing a hundred times – so you give it to them. Clean, fast, correct. They thank you and leave. It felt like help, and it was, a little.

It was also a small withdrawal from the one account you most need to keep growing: their ability to solve it themselves next time.

The urge to answer is the signal

Here’s the reframe that changes everything, once you can feel it: the urge to give the answer is the signal to ask a question. That pull to grab the wheel – oh, I know this one – almost always fires at the exact moment the person in front of you was about to find the answer themselves. Your certainty is the tell. The faster you know the answer, the more it’s probably their rep to take, not yours.

This is the Coach gear in VOICES – leading through questions instead of answers. Not therapy, not withholding for its own sake. Just the discipline of asking “What have you tried?” or “What do you think the move is?” before you reach for “Here’s what I’d do.” The question keeps the problem in their hands long enough for them to grow a grip on it.

What a question does that an answer can’t

An answer solves today’s problem. A question builds the person who’ll solve the next ten without you. When you tell, the solution is yours – borrowed, not owned – and they come back for the next one, because why wouldn’t they? You’re faster. When you ask, they do the cognitive work, and the judgment that work builds stays in their head after they leave your office. One exchange makes you indispensable. The other makes you replaceable, which is the entire goal.

Caption: Telling vs coaching – an answer solves today; a question builds the person who solves the next ten.

Slower this week, faster the week you’re out

There’s a reason high-control leaders skip this gear, and it isn’t that they don’t care. It’s that coaching is genuinely slower in the moment, and a busy leader optimizes for the moment. Asking takes longer than telling. Watching someone work toward an answer you already have is mildly excruciating. So you tell, and you tell, and you stay the fastest path to every answer – which is exactly why nothing moves when you’re gone.

The Coach gear is a trade: a little slower this week, in exchange for a team that doesn’t need you next quarter. You won’t always have time for it – a real crisis wants Command, not a coaching question. But on the ordinary Tuesday problems, the ones where there’s time and the stakes are survivable, the question is the highest-leverage thing you can do, because you’re not just solving a problem. You’re building someone who solves problems.

Worth sitting with this week:

  • Where did you give an answer this week that someone was one good question away from finding themselves?

  • What’s your tell – the feeling, right before you grab the wheel, that you could learn to read as “ask, don’t tell”?

  • Who would grow fastest if you spent a month asking them instead of answering them?
