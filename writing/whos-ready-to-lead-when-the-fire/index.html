Who's ready to lead when the fire hits?

Resolution #4: Stop Absorbing the Shock – Start Building the Bench

Matt Goode12 December 20256 min read Get new pieces by email

# My role is changing# DELEGATE

“If the system only works when you absorb the shock, it’s not scaling – it’s straining.”

Two months ago, on a coaching call, a founder told me something I’ve heard more times than I can count:

“I know I need to build my leadership bench – but right now isn’t a good time,”

I asked him when a good time might be.

He laughed. Paused. Then said quietly:

“Probably after the next fire.”

Which is – of course – exactly when it’s too late.

This is Resolution #4 in my series on building a business that holds its shape. [Previous: Speed Isn’t About Work Ethic. It’s About Decision Clarity.]

Because by the time the fire hits, the bench you needed is the bench you didn’t build. And the shock you’re absorbing is the shock the system wasn’t designed to handle.

The Business That Ran on One Person’s Exhaustion

That founder’s company was already burning – he couldn’t see the smoke yet.

Three months earlier, I’d met one of his managers in a BootCamp cohort. Talented. Respected. High-capacity. Precisely the kind of person who should have been part of his leadership bench.

I asked her a simple question: “What decisions are fully yours?”

She looked confused.

“I’m not actually sure. I think I’m… assisting leadership more than being leadership.”

She was performing the role without the recognition. Absorbing pressure without the authority. Carrying responsibility she didn’t technically own. Waiting to be told she was ready – while quietly proving it every day.

And her founder? He wasn’t failing her intentionally.

He was doing something far more common – waiting for leaders to emerge instead of preparing them to lead.

Six months later, she left.

Not for more money. Not for a fancy title. Not for a better office.

She left for a company where someone saw her as a leader before she had to earn it through struggle.

Where the bench wasn’t accidental. Where leadership wasn’t a reward. Where the system made space for her to grow before the fire demanded it.

The Silent Warning Sign

Here’s the line I hear constantly from founders:

“My team is great – they just need more time.”

No.

Great teams don’t need more time. They need more people capable of carrying the load.

Because when every escalation loops back to you… When every crisis waits for your context… When every final decision needs your blessing…

You haven’t scaled leadership. You’ve scaled dependency.

And dependency always snaps under pressure.

Not because your team is weak. But because the system has quietly trained them to rely on the one person whose bandwidth will always run out first: you.

Building vs. Triaging

There’s a difference between building leaders and triaging crises.

Building looks like: Coaching someone through a decision they could’ve made alone.

Triaging looks like: Making the decision yourself because there’s no time to explain it.

One creates capacity. The other drains yours.

Most founders tell themselves they’re building when they’re actually just surviving.

You don’t build the bench:

  • When someone is already overwhelmed

  • When the team is already stretched thin

  • When you’re already drowning

  • When the stakes are already high

By then, you’re not building. You’re compensating.

The companies that scale smoothly? They pre-load leadership capacity long before the crisis demands it. They build altitude into the system early – so when the pressure hits, the structure holds.

The Bench Depth Test

Here’s the diagnostic I use with founders:

If you stepped away for two weeks, who would handle:

  • Client escalations

  • Team performance issues

  • Conflicting priorities

  • Stalled projects

  • Role disputes

  • Cross-functional coordination

  • High-stakes decisions

If you can’t name at least two people who could carry these essentials, your bench isn’t ready.

That doesn’t mean you’ve failed. It means you’re building the business on a single beam.

And single beams crack under stress.

If you can name them? You’ve built redundancy where it matters most – not in processes, not in playbooks, but in leadership capacity.

You’ve built a system that can absorb pressure without pulling you back into the center every time something shakes.

Want to map your leadership bench? I use a Leadership Bench Depth Audit with every founder I coach. It reveals who’s genuinely ready now, who could be ready with targeted coaching, and where your bench is thin. Reply “send it” and I’ll share it.

What It Looks Like When the Bench Actually Works

Last quarter, a founder I work with took a two-week vacation – his first real time off in three years.

When he got back, I asked him how many fires he had to put out.

“None,” he said. Then after a pause: “Actually… I think they forgot I was gone.”

That’s not abandonment. That’s the bench working.

Decisions got made without hesitation because leaders knew exactly what they owned. Stress is distributed rather than bottlenecked because the system no longer relies on one person’s stamina. And for the first time in years, he got something back that money can’t buy:

Headspace.

Not because he was working less – but because he was no longer the only person strong enough to hold the weight.

This is what scaling actually feels like. Not bigger. Lighter.

Your Identity Shift for 2026

This resolution isn’t about becoming a better “delegator.”

It’s about becoming someone who builds leaders before the fire demands them.

It’s the difference between walking into a meeting thinking “I need to handle this” versus “Who on my team could handle this with the right coaching?”

Same situation. Different operating system.

That identity creates structure. Structure creates capacity. Capacity creates headspace.

Because once you see yourself as someone who pre-loads leadership capacity, everything downstream becomes cleaner, lighter, and more sustainable.

Your team stops waiting for your blessing. Your calendar stops filling with decisions that shouldn’t need you. Your nervous system stops treating every week like crisis management.

And your business stops depending on your durability.

The Question That Changes Everything

So here’s what I want you to ask yourself:

Is your business actually scaling – or are you the one absorbing the shock?

Because shock absorption has a shelf life. Your stamina does. Your calendar does. Your nervous system definitely does.

Leadership capacity doesn’t. It compounds. It distributes weight. It creates stability that doesn’t hinge on your presence, your energy, or your heroics.

If the system only works when you cushion every impact, that’s not resilience – it’s deferred fragility.

Scaling starts the moment your business stops depending on your durability and starts depending on its own.

Take 30 seconds: Who on your team could step into a leadership role tomorrow if you gave them the authority and coaching? Drop it in the comments – I read every one.

Your 2026 Resolution

Say it out loud: “I will build leaders before I need them – because if I wait until the fire is already burning, I’m not building. I’m surviving.”

This resolution sticks because identity drives behavior. Not goals. Not intentions. Identity.

Once you see yourself as someone who pre-loads leadership capacity, your decisions start aligning with that reality automatically.

This isn’t idealism. It’s operational sanity.

Your business will never grow faster than your people.

If your team isn’t actively preparing for the next version of the company, you will always be the one compensating for the gap. Always the shock absorber. Always the contingency plan. Always the person holding more weight than the system was designed to require.

Future capacity isn’t something you discover in a crisis. It’s something you build quietly, deliberately, before the pressure hits.
