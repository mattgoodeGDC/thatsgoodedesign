The Engine Most Leaders Never Tune

Matt Goode16 June 20262 min read Get new pieces by email

# CREATE

Before I worked with companies, I spent time around humanitarian operations, the kind of work where the strain on a system is never theoretical. The crises aren’t what stayed with me. What stayed with me was watching good organizations, full of people who cared enormously, come apart under pressure they were never built to hold.

The talent was there. The mission was there. What gave way was the wiring underneath: who owned what, who had the authority to act, whether people trusted each other enough to move fast when there was no time to check. The caring never stopped. The structure beneath the caring was never engineered for a bad day.

The engine you forgot you had

That structure has a name in CLUTCH. CREATE is the engine your leadership runs on, and it has six cylinders.

Culture is the climate you set. Relationships is the trust you can actually draw on. Execution is whether work finishes or just moves. Agility is how fast you adapt when the plan breaks. Teaching is whether people are more capable because of how you lead them. Ethics is what holds when the easy call and the right call split.

Caption: CREATE’s six cylinders – the engine your leadership runs on.

Most leaders pour everything into the two or three cylinders they’re already good at and let the rest idle. The operator floors Execution and lets Relationships go cold. The visionary keeps Culture humming and never builds the systems Execution needs. The engine still turns. It just isn’t balanced, and an unbalanced engine grinds down the people riding in it.

The cold cylinder always sends an invoice

And it’s not a soft cost. A cold Relationships cylinder shows up as the manager you didn’t know was unhappy until the resignation lands, at something like $65,000 to recruit and ramp a replacement, plus the months of half-speed while the seat is warm but empty. A cold Teaching cylinder shows up as a bench too thin to cover for you, which is the actual reason you can’t take the two weeks. A cold Ethics cylinder shows up once, catastrophically, in a decision you wish you could take back.

The instinct, when something feels off, is to add horsepower to what you’re already good at, because that’s where it feels good to push. It’s the wrong move. The leverage is in the cylinder you stopped checking. Find that one, warm it up, and the whole engine smooths out.

The tell is usually a story you’ve stopped questioning. “Relationships sort themselves out.” “No time to teach right now.” “Ethics isn’t really an issue here.” Each one is a cylinder you decided to stop checking, and the decision is invisible to you precisely because you made it so long ago. The diagnostic works by making you say it out loud.

Worth asking, not answering quickly:

  • Which cylinder carries most of your leadership right now?

  • Which one have you let go cold, and what has that cost you that you never connected to it?

  • If you tuned one cylinder this quarter, which would the people around you feel first?
