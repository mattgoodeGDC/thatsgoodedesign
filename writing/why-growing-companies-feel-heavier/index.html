Why Growing Companies Feel Heavier at High Speeds

A Relativity Lesson for Leaders

Matt Goode1 December 20252 min read Get new pieces by email

# Growing faster than it's getting clearer

Introduction to the Series: The Architecture of Scale

There’s a counter-intuitive truth at the heart of physics that every founder learns eventually – not in a lab, but in their calendar.

In Einstein’s world, the faster an object moves, the heavier it becomes.
Velocity increases mass.
Speed creates weight.

In scaling companies, the same phenomenon plays out every day.

What begins as light, fast, exhilarating momentum slowly becomes something denser – decisions take longer, clarity gets harder to maintain, and the organization starts to feel strangely heavy even as the numbers look good. Leaders often misread this heaviness as a personal failing:
If I were better at delegating…
If the team were more aligned…
If I just pushed a little harder…

But the weight isn’t a sign that you’re doing something wrong.
It’s a sign that you’re moving fast enough for the physics of scale to show up.

Just like in relativity, speed exposes architecture.

In the early stage, your clarity and proximity act like a gravitational field – keeping everything aligned simply because you’re close. But as speed increases, that early operating model hits its limit. The system becomes heavier than the structure designed to carry it.

And the instinctive moves leaders make in response – stepping in, reorganizing, accelerating harder – often increase the weight instead of redistributing it.

This series is about that weight.

It’s about the moment every scaling leader feels but rarely names:
Why does everything still come through me?
Why does speed feel slower now?
Why does the team need more clarity the more we grow?

Over the next five essays, I’m going to break down the real architecture of scale – the operating systems, decision pathways, rhythms, and design choices that determine whether a company becomes lighter as it grows…or collapses under its own speed.

Here’s where we’ll go:

1️⃣ The Hidden Breakpoint in Scaling Teams
Why the early operating system eventually collapses – and how to spot the invisible ceiling before you hit it.

2️⃣ The Heroic Trap
How stepping in feels responsible but slowly centralizes fragility and creates learned dependence.

3️⃣ The Architecture Beneath the Org Chart
Why reorgs fail – and why behavior changes only when architecture changes.

4️⃣ The Hidden Drag of Fast Teams
Why speed without structure becomes chaos – and how high-performing teams actually accelerate.

5️⃣ Freedom by Design
How to build a company that moves forward when you step back – optionality without irrelevance.

Each essay is practical, rooted in research (HBR, McKinsey, MIT Sloan, Edmondson), and focused on one core idea:

Scale isn’t a function of effort.
It’s a function of architecture.

If your company has reached the point where speed feels heavy, where decisions are clustering around you, or where the team is growing faster than the system built to support it, this series will help you see the underlying structures clearly – and give you the language and tools to rebuild them with intention.

Because in leadership, just like in physics, speed doesn’t have to create weight.
Not if the architecture is designed to hold it.

Let’s get into it.
