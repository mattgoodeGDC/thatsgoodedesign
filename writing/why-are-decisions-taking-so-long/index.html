Why are decisions taking so long?

Resolution #3: Speed Isn’t About Work Ethic. It’s About Decision Clarity.

Matt Goode11 December 20256 min read Get new pieces by email

# 6C

“Speed is rarely about work ethic. It’s almost always about decision clarity.”

Two months ago, at a leadership retreat, I watched a team spend 22 minutes debating a decision that should’ve taken two.

Everyone spoke. Everyone nodded. Everyone re-explained their position. Everyone kept glancing at the founder.

They weren’t confused. They weren’t cautious. They weren’t lacking context.

They were stuck because no one knew who was supposed to decide.

This is Resolution #3 in my series on building a business that holds its shape. [Previous: If Your Team Can’t Name Your Priorities, You Don’t Have Any]

That wasn’t indecision. That was decision friction – and it’s one of the highest hidden costs inside scaling companies.

People assume slow execution is a motivation problem. It’s not.

It’s a cognitive load problem.

Because when people have to calculate mentally:

  • Who actually owns this

  • How this kind of decision should be made

  • Whose input is required

  • Who needs to be informed

  • And when it’s “safe” to act

…they hesitate.

Not because they’re incapable. But because the system is ambiguous.

Ambiguity creates drag. Drag kills speed. And speed is a systems problem long before it’s a people problem.

What Decision Friction Actually Feels Like

Two patterns show up in almost every company:

The Silent Delay

A PM keeps a decision in her inbox for three days.

Not because she’s avoiding it – but because she genuinely doesn’t know if it’s her call, her VP’s call, or the founder’s call.

So she waits. Not from fear – from ambiguity.

The “Just to Be Safe” Escalation

A senior leader forwards everything upward because no one ever defined what autonomy actually means at their level.

“Safe” becomes code for: “I’m unclear.”

These aren’t performance issues. They’re system issues masquerading as people issues.

And until the system gets clearer, no amount of coaching, feedback, or motivation will fix the drag.

The Retreat Moment That Changed a Company

During a retreat last year, I asked a leadership team to sort their recent decisions into categories.

We dumped dozens of sticky notes onto the table – hiring, budgets, product choices, pricing, client escalations.

Then I asked: “Which of these were yours to decide – and which should not have been?”

Silence.

Then one leader finally said, “I didn’t know we were supposed to decide some of these.”

Another added: “I didn’t know we were allowed to decide these.”

The founder went quiet, staring at the table covered in color-coded confusion.

After a long moment, he exhaled:

“I thought I was empowering them. Turns out I was just… hoping they’d figure it out.”

We rebuilt their entire decision system that afternoon.

By the next quarter, they weren’t guessing anymore. They were moving.

Enter the 6C Decision Framework (Your Team’s New Operating System)

Here’s the backbone of decision clarity – your 6C Model:

1. Command A leader decides unilaterally. Use when speed matters more than input: “We’re launching Friday.”

Command isn’t domineering. It’s decisive – when time is the constraint.

2. Consultative A decider seeks input, then chooses. Use when you need perspective, not permission.

This is the sweet spot for most strategic calls.

3. Collaborative A small group co-creates the decision. Best for interdependent work across functions.

Collaboration here is structured, not a free-for-all.

4. Consensus The team collectively agrees. Rare. Expensive. Intentional.

Reserve it for values-level or identity-level choices – the things you need everyone to own, not just follow.

5. Core Expertise The decision belongs to the expert, not the most senior person. “If it’s legal, legal decides. If it’s design, design decides.”

This distributes judgment where it actually lives.

6. Choice Low-stakes decisions anyone can make autonomously. No approvals. No bottlenecks. No waiting.

Choice decisions keep the system moving without clogging leadership bandwidth.

The 6Cs aren’t bureaucracy. They’re cognitive unburdening. They remove the guesswork that slows companies down.

When people know how a decision should be made and who owns the call, their hesitation disappears – and speed returns.

Want to map your decisions to the 6Cs? I use a Decision Quick Card with every leadership team I coach. It clarifies who decides, how the decision gets made, and what can move autonomously. Reply “send it,” and I’ll share it.

Why This Isn’t Bureaucracy – It’s Cognitive Unburdening

People don’t need more authority to move fast. They need more certainty.

When every decision is placed inside one of the 6Cs, the ambiguity tax disappears.

People stop wondering if they can decide and start executing how they already know how to decide.

Execution gets cleaner. Escalations drop. Speed becomes predictable.

Because once the system removes the guesswork, the team stops hesitating and starts moving.

The “After” Picture – Three Months Later

This is what the founder told me a quarter later:

  • Meetings were 30–40% shorter

  • Escalations dropped dramatically

  • Decisions got made at the right level

  • The team moved faster without added pressure

  • He stopped being the bottleneck

And here’s the part that mattered most:

Nothing else had changed. Not the people. Not the strategy. Not the goals.

Just this: The team finally knew how decisions were made.

Once the system carried the clarity, they no longer needed to look to the founder for permission, interpretation, or direction.

Speed didn’t come from working harder. It came from removing the guesswork.

Your Identity Shift for 2026

This resolution isn’t about becoming a more “decisive” leader.

It’s about becoming someone who removes the guesswork so your team can move without hesitation.

It’s the difference between walking into a Monday meeting asking, “Why is this taking so long?” versus “Which decisions are still unclear?”

Same problem. Different operating system.

That identity creates structure. Structure creates clarity. Clarity creates speed.

Because once you see yourself as the leader who eliminates decision friction, everything downstream becomes cleaner, lighter, and faster.

Your team stops hesitating. Your meetings stop circling. Your decisions stop boomeranging.

And execution stops depending on you being in the room.

The Two-Minute Decision Audit

Ask yourself:

“How many decisions come back to me not because they should – but because the process is unclear?”

If the answer is anything above “a few,” your people aren’t slow. Your system is.

Because decisions don’t boomerang upward due to incompetence. They boomerang because the architecture hasn’t told people who owns what, how calls get made, or when autonomy actually applies.

Fix the system, and the boomerangs stop. Fix the clarity, and the speed returns.

Take 30 seconds: What’s the one decision that keeps coming back to you that shouldn’t? Drop it in the comments – I read every one.

Your 2026 Resolution

Say it out loud: “I will eliminate decision friction.”

This resolution sticks because identity drives behavior. Not goals. Not intentions. Identity.

Once you see yourself as someone who removes guesswork, your decisions automatically align with that reality.

No extra hours. No extra meetings. No heroic effort.

Just deliberate clarity – the kind that speeds up every corner of your business because people finally know how to decide, when to decide, and who decides what.

This isn’t about working harder. It’s about designing a system that moves faster because ambiguity isn’t dragging it down.
