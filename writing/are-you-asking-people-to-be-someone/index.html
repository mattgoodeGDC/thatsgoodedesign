Resolution #9: Stop Asking People to Be Someone They’re Not

Matt Goode19 December 20258 min read Get new pieces by email

# Neuroinclusion

“You hired people for their strengths – then asked them to work against type.”

A few months ago, during a CliftonStrengths workshop, a team member said something that hit the room like truth dropped from a height:

“I think I’ve been evaluated for traits I don’t actually have.”

The room went silent.

She didn’t say it with frustration. She said it with relief – like she finally understood the friction she’d been carrying for years.

Her manager looked at her: “What do you mean?”

“I’m Strategic and Ideation,” she said. “But my performance reviews focus on whether I’m consistent with follow-through. That’s not where I’m wired to shine.”

The manager paused.

“Oh. I’ve been evaluating you against my strengths, not yours.”

This is Resolution #9 in my series on building a business that holds its shape. [Previous: Role Ambiguity Creates Friendly Fire – Not Collaboration]

Exactly.

This is the quiet cost inside so many organizations: People lose energy not because the work is hard, but because the work isn’t aligned with how they naturally think, feel, and operate.

Competence Is Not the Same as Energy

Here’s what most leaders miss:

Someone can be good at something and still be drained by it.

That’s not a strength. That’s compensation.

Strengths aren’t about what you CAN do. They’re about what you do with natural efficiency and energy.

When teams work with their strengths:

  • Decisions happen faster

  • Collaboration becomes smoother

  • Problems get solved at higher altitude

  • Stress becomes manageable

When teams work against their strengths:

  • Resistance increases

  • Burnout accelerates

  • Every task feels heavier than it should

Friction isn’t always a workflow problem. Sometimes it’s a talent alignment problem.

The High-Talent Team Running in Sand

Last year, I ran a strengths workshop for a team that was stacked with raw talent:

Strategic thinkers. Activators. Relators. Analysts. Communicators. Organizers.

If you looked at their profiles, you’d assume they were unstoppable.

But in practice, they felt stuck. Tired. Out of sync.

When we mapped their strengths to their actual responsibilities, it became obvious why:

Their Strategic people were stuck in reactive work – doing what they were “good at” but what drained them.

Their Relators were buried in spreadsheets – legacy responsibilities no one had revisited.

Their Executors were waiting on collaboration-heavy tasks – because the founder’s style was consultative, and everyone mirrored it.

Their big-picture thinkers had nowhere to aim the vision.

Their detail-oriented people were playing cleanup at the end of projects instead of being involved upfront where their precision actually mattered.

The founder looked at the board and said:

“I hired them right. I deployed them wrong.”

That’s the moment everything shifted.

The Redesign

We spent the afternoon redesigning responsibilities around their natural wiring.

The Strategic thinkers moved to quarterly planning instead of daily operations. The Relators shifted to client relationships instead of spreadsheets. The Executors got clear ownership of time-sensitive deliverables – no more waiting on endless input loops. The big-picture thinkers took ownership of long-range strategy sessions. The detail-oriented people moved upstream to project design, not just cleanup.

Same people. Same business. Different deployment.

Six weeks later, the founder told me:

“We didn’t add headcount. We didn’t work faster. We just stopped fighting people’s wiring.”

What Strategic Deployment Actually Looks Like

Here’s a concrete example:

Take someone with high Strategic.

Bad deployment:

  • Daily task management

  • Routine operations

  • Reactive problem-solving

Good deployment:

  • Quarterly planning

  • Scenario modeling

  • Identifying patterns across projects

Take someone with high Relator.

Bad deployment:

  • Data analysis

  • Process documentation

  • Solo execution work

Good deployment:

  • Client relationship management

  • Team cohesion building

  • Cross-functional coordination

Same person. Radically different energy output.

The Hidden Cost of Working Against Type

Working against type doesn’t just slow execution – it quietly erodes confidence.

People start to think they’re not good at their jobs when really, they’re just not wired for the jobs they’ve been given.

That’s not a performance problem. That’s a deployment problem.

And it’s expensive:

Energy drain – People compensating using strengths no one names or recognizes

Invisible labor – Good performers working twice as hard to produce half the results

Quiet attrition – Your best people leave because they never got to work in their genius zones

Where Strengths Misalignment Hides

1. “Good at it” ≠ Strength Competence without energy is compensation, not contribution.

2. Legacy responsibilities People still doing things they’ve outgrown or never enjoyed.

3. The founder’s preferences Teams unintentionally mirror the founder’s style – even when it doesn’t fit them.

4. The wrong success metrics Evaluating big-picture thinkers on detail perfection. Or relationship-builders on rapid solo execution.

Once you see it, you can fix it.

Want to map your team’s strengths to their roles? I’ve built a Strengths Deployment Guide that identifies misalignment and helps you redesign responsibilities around natural wiring. Reply “send it” and I’ll share it.

The Diagnostic

Here’s the test to identify misalignment on your team:

Ask each team member:

  • “What part of your job energizes you?”

  • “What part drains you?”

  • “If you could redesign your role, what would you shift?”

If more than 30% of someone’s role is in the “drains me” column, you have a deployment problem.

Not a performance problem. A deployment problem.

The “After” Picture

Three months after their strengths overhaul, that team sent me an email:

“Our Strategic leads stopped feeling like they were drowning in tactical requests. Our Relators stopped dreading spreadsheet reviews. Our Executors stopped waiting for endless input loops.

We didn’t work faster. The work just stopped fighting us.”

That’s the difference.

Strengths-aligned teams:

  • Collaborate faster

  • Make cleaner decisions

  • Stay in their genius zones

  • Communicate more naturally

  • Produce more with stress

  • Bounce back from setbacks faster

They don’t burn out at the same rate. They don’t conflict at the same frequency. They don’t question themselves as often.

They’re built for altitude.

This Is Where Role Clarity Becomes Powerful

This is where your role clarity from Resolution #8 becomes powerful:

You’ve defined who owns what. Now you need to ensure the “who” is naturally suited for the “what.”

Role clarity + strengths alignment = execution without friction.

Your Identity Shift for 2026

This resolution isn’t about becoming a better “manager of people.”

It’s about becoming someone who stops asking people to compensate – and starts putting them where they naturally excel.

It’s the difference between walking into a performance review thinking “Why isn’t this person meeting expectations?” versus “Are we asking this person to work against type?”

Same underperformance. Different operating system.

That identity creates structure. Structure creates alignment. Alignment creates energy.

Because once you see yourself as someone who deploys talent strategically, everything downstream becomes cleaner, lighter, and more sustainable.

Your team stops compensating. Your culture stops burning people out on misaligned work. Your organization stops losing good people to bad deployment.

And your best performers finally get to work in their genius zones.

The Question That Changes Everything

Here’s what I want you to ask yourself:

How much of your team’s “underperformance” is actually just misalignment?

Because if you’re evaluating people against strengths they don’t have, you’re not measuring performance – you’re measuring compensation.

And compensation always has a shelf life.

Take 30 seconds: Who on your team might be compensating rather than contributing from their strengths? Drop it in the comments – I read every one.

Your 2026 Resolution

Say it out loud: “I will stop asking people to compensate – and start putting them where they naturally excel.”

This resolution sticks because identity drives behavior. Not goals. Not intentions. Identity.

Once you see yourself as someone who deploys people strategically, your decisions automatically align with that reality.

Not casually. Not occasionally. Not when HR asks for it. Strategically.

This is what I’ve spent years helping leaders do with CliftonStrengths and strengths-based deployment frameworks:

Align strengths to responsibilities – so teams work in their genius zones, not their compensation zones.

You’ve already hired the right people. Now put them where they can actually lead.
