Why Leadership Training Doesn't Stick

Matt Goode26 June 20263 min read Get new pieces by email

# Winning feels like a near miss# Coaching

You’ve seen this movie. The offsite is great – an energizing speaker, a clean framework, sticky notes everywhere, and real insight in the room. People leave fired up. The binder goes on the shelf. And three weeks later, nothing has changed: the same manager is still avoiding the same hard conversation, still running the same meeting the same way.

It’s tempting to blame the content or the people. Usually, it’s neither. It’s the form.

Training is an event. Development is a practice.

A workshop is an event – a fixed block of time where information gets transferred. Development is a practice – reps, over time, with feedback, until a behavior becomes a default. We keep funding the first and calling it the second – a training line when the thing we want needs a capital plan: designed, maintained, never quite finished – then act surprised when capability doesn’t show up. Leadership isn’t something you can be told. It’s something you get better at by doing, badly at first, with someone close enough to coach the next attempt.

Why the event fades

There’s a reason the binder doesn’t stick, and it isn’t motivation. Memory decays fast without retrieval – Ebbinghaus mapped the forgetting curve more than a century ago, and a single exposure, however good, sits right on it. Worse, knowing a framework and being able to use one under pressure are different skills entirely. You can recite the steps of a hard conversation the afternoon of the workshop and still freeze in the real one on Thursday, because what you practiced was understanding, not doing.

What actually builds people

John Wooden, the most successful coach in the history of college basketball, almost never gave speeches about winning. He ran reps. Footwork, fundamentals, the same small things over and over, until they were automatic and his players could improvise on top of them under championship pressure. He built judgment the only way judgment gets built: in the doing, corrected in real time.

Marcus Aurelius named the law two thousand years before that gym existed: you don’t rise to the occasion, you fall to the level of your preparation. A workshop raises the ceiling of what someone understands. It never moves the floor they fall to – and the floor is the only part that shows up on Thursday.

That’s the shift for anyone serious about developing people. Frameworks don’t develop people. What you do inside them does. The framework – CREATE, VOICES, the 6C Compass, any of them – isn’t the lesson; it’s the field you practice on.

And the reps have an order. CLUTCH calls it the DELEGATE handoff, and it runs in three stages.

  • First, the Ride-Along: you make the call while they watch, and say the quiet part out loud – the judgment a veteran makes without noticing they’re making it.

  • Then the Learner’s Permit: they drive, and you coach from beside them, close enough to catch a real mistake.

  • Then Solo Drive: you get out of the car, the decision is theirs, the stakes are real, and you find out what they actually built. That’s where development lives – in the wobble, in the debrief that turns one decision into judgment they keep – not in the binder.

If you coach for a living, this is the line for the client who asks you for a workshop: I can give you the event, or I can build you the practice. One of them is over by Friday. The other one is still working a year from now.

That’s the case for the slow version. You can’t shortcut a skill into someone in an afternoon, and the afternoon doesn’t compound. The reps do – every coached decision is a deposit, and a year of them is a person who is measurably better, which is more than any binder has ever returned.

Worth sitting with this week:

  • What development are you funding as an event that only works as a practice?

  • Where could you turn a real decision this week into a coached rep instead of a quick answer?

  • A year from now, who on your team will be measurably better – and what reps will have gotten them there?
