What’s Really Driving Your Team?

A 20-Minute Culture Reflection for New Leaders

Matt Goode14 June 20252 min read Get new pieces by email

# CREATE# The culture is drifting

Culture isn’t declared. It’s absorbed.

It lives in tone, silence, and repetition – especially when no one’s looking.

For new leaders, especially in fast-moving teams, there’s often no time to reflect – just execute. However, executing without awareness can quietly reinforce norms that may not serve your team.

That’s where the New Leader Culture Pulse Worksheet comes in.

This 20-minute reflection tool helps you:

  • Identify the unspoken patterns that shape your team

  • Notice what behaviors are reinforcing culture (good or bad)

  • Name what might need intentional change

This Week’s Coaching Prompt

“What’s quietly running the culture here – and do I want to reinforce or reshape it?”

When to Use It

One new manager I worked with noticed that her loudest team member was shaping nearly every decision. Her Culture Pulse reflection helped her spot a consistent silence from others, and within one week, she shifted her meeting structure to invite broader voices.

This tool is built for moments like that. When you’re too busy to notice, but you know something feels… off.

Insights from The Culture Code by Daniel Coyle

Use these principles to deepen your worksheet reflection:

1. Safety is the foundation.
Psychological safety isn’t a soft concept – it’s the base layer of every high-functioning team.

Ask yourself: “When mistakes happen, what’s my first reaction?”

2. Vulnerability precedes trust.
Great cultures emerge when leaders lead with honesty.

Consider: Share one of your worksheet answers with your team. Model reflection. Invite openness.

3. Culture is built through small, repeated signals.
What you notice, question, or let slide becomes the code your team follows.

Ask: “What am I unintentionally signaling?”

4. Belonging cues shape connection.
Tiny behaviors – eye contact, inclusive language, check-ins – signal who belongs.

Go deeper: “Who stays silent?” Add: “Are they receiving signals that they’re safe and valued?”

Your 20-Minute Culture Pulse Worksheet

Set a timer. Reflect honestly. Then, if safe, test your insights with a peer or team member. Culture change starts with visibility.

Ask yourself:

  • What behaviors do I quietly reward?

  • When mistakes happen, what’s my first reaction?

  • Who do I really hear in meetings – and who do I overlook?

  • What do we celebrate, and how do we celebrate it?

  • What are the unwritten rules for decision-making?

  • What’s one thing that’s never talked about – but clearly matters?

Use this reflection to align with your values, challenge invisible assumptions, and spark the kind of small conversations that lead to real change.

Your culture is already speaking. It’s time to listen – and lead.

Try It This Week

  • Set a timer. Answer honestly. Then share one insight with a colleague.

  • Do you know a peer manager learning to lead with greater awareness? Forward this to them.
