Occupation Without Representation: The Urgent Case for DC Statehood

Wear a T-Shirt, its better than throwing Subs

Matt Goode28 August 20252 min read Get new pieces by email

# Off the clock

I’ve always believed a t-shirt can start a conversation.

Years ago, I designed my own “Dyslexic” font and made shirts for myself, my kids, and a few friends – celebrating dyslexia as a superpower. Folks would come up to me in the grocery store, and ask me about it, or give me a thumbs up, or share, “Hey, I’m dyslexic too!”

This latest design carries another message that needs to be said out loud: Taxation without representation is un-American.

When I was RIF’ed from USAID in February, one of my first instincts – after selling off my Tesla stock – was to call my representatives in Congress. A teammate quietly reminded me: “At least you have a congressman to call.” 

That landed. Hard.

Washington, DC, is home to over 700,000 Americans – more than the populations of Vermont and Wyoming combined. Its economy, at $190 billion, is larger than those of Alaska, Vermont, Wyoming, Idaho, New Mexico, and Mississippi. 

Pardon a brief digression to highlight the contrast between DC and Mississippi, since the Mississippi National Guard is one of the units deployed to DC at the moment under the Trump Administration.

  • Mississippi’s capital is named after Andrew Jackson, the man behind the infamous Indian Removal Act. Washington, DC is named after George. 

  • DC's average household income is double that of Mississippi ($108K vs. $54K), with a lower poverty rate (13.6% vs. 19.5%). 

  • DC is also far more educated – 64% of adults hold a bachelor’s degree compared to 24% in Mississippi

  • While DC faces serious public safety challenges, Mississippi has a higher gun-death rate (29.6 vs. 21.4 per 100K). 

By most measures, DC looks more like a thriving state than the Magnola State. Just saying.

Yet residents have no voting representation in Congress. And because of that unique status, DC has repeatedly been treated as less than a state. The aforementioned troops have occupied the city – not by the consent of its people or local leadership, but at the direction of outsiders. 

But make no mistake – DC is not a bureaucratic anomaly. It’s vibrant and fully American: the birthplace of Go-Go music, home to over 15 universities, and one of only five states in the U.S. (along with California, NY, Florida, Minnesota, and Illinois) that fields professional teams in the NHL, MLB, NBA, WNBA, and MLS. Sorry, NFL Commanders, you’re close, but come back to RFK and I’ll add you to the list. 

Its residents pay taxes, serve in the military, and contribute fully to the nation. By every measure of fairness, democracy, and representation, DC has earned the right to be the 51st state.

The DC flag, with its bold red bars and three stars, is one of the most recognizable city flags in the world – adapted from George Washington’s family coat of arms. BTW, it also appears on the US military Purple Heart ICYWW. It's probably only second to Chicago as one of the best city flags out there. But it should be a state flag. 

My t-shirt design builds on that imagery, pairing a symbol of local pride with a clear message – it’s a statement about democracy and the unfinished business of making DC the 51st state.
