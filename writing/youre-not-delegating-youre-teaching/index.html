You're Not Delegating. You're Teaching Them to Drive.

Matt Goode16 July 20263 min read Get new pieces by email

# DELEGATE# Everything runs through me

I spent two decades in classrooms before I ever set foot in a boardroom, and the lesson that carried over cleanest was this: you cannot hand someone judgment in a single sitting.

You can hand them information. You can hand them a task. Judgment is different. It forms in the reps, in the stretch where they make the call and you’re close enough to catch the fall but far enough to let it be real. A teacher who lectures and then tests has taught nothing about how to think. The same is true of a leader who explains the job once and then expects ownership.

Delegation transfers a task. Development builds a driver.

You build a driver in stages

And you don’t build a driver by tossing a teenager the keys and a destination. You build one in stages, and the order isn’t optional.

The Ride-Along comes first. You drive, they watch, and the point is to make your thinking visible. You narrate the reasoning behind the call, not just the call. They learn how you read the road before they ever touch the wheel.

Then the Learner’s Permit. Now they drive, you’re in the passenger seat, and this is where most of the real growth happens. You ask instead of grab. You let them work the problem while you sit on your hands, which is harder than it sounds. The 6C Compass becomes a teaching tool here: “This one’s yours to call. Here’s why I’m not making it for you.”

Last is the Solo Drive. They own it. You’ve moved from instructor to occasional passenger, close enough to coach, far enough to let it become theirs.

Caption: You build a driver in three stages – and the middle is the one most leaders skip.

The skipped stage is the expensive one

Most leaders compress this. They go from Ride-Along straight to Solo and call it trust, then wonder why their people freeze or crash. The missing middle is where it costs you, because the Learner’s Permit is the only place judgment is built, and judgment is the one thing you can’t cover for when you’re out.

Think about what a single real driver is worth to you. Not a competent task-doer – a driver, someone who can take the next decision off your desk and the one after that, and grow the person beneath them. One of those is worth more than a year of you working weekends, because they multiply while you only ever add. Development is the only line of work where the payoff is your own freedom.

A caution worth holding: development is slow on purpose, and slow is exactly what an overloaded leader can’t stand. So you grab the wheel “just this once,” and just this once becomes the pattern, and a year on you’re still the only driver. The investment feels like a tax in the moment. It’s the only thing that ever actually lightens the load.

Look at the people you’re growing, and ask:

  • Which stage is each of them actually in, and which stage are you treating them as if they’re in?

  • Where have you jumped someone to Solo and called it empowerment?

  • What becomes possible for you in a year if even one of them becomes a true driver?
