Does your week run on rhythm or chaos?

Resolution #7: Stop Setting Goals – Start Building Rhythms

Matt Goode17 December 20256 min read Get new pieces by email

# Winning feels like a near miss

“You don’t rise to the level of your goals. You fall to the level of your systems.” – James Clear

A founder told me recently, “We set clear goals every quarter. We just... don’t hit them.”

I asked: “What happens between the goal-setting and the deadline?”

Long pause.

“We... work on stuff?”

Exactly the problem.

So I asked him a different question: “What are your operating rhythms?”

He blinked. Paused.

“We meet when we need to.”

This is Resolution #7 in my series on building a business that holds its shape. [Previous: High Standards Need High Safety – or They Break People]

That’s the leadership equivalent of saying you’ll “work out when you feel like it.”

There is no performance without a pattern. No consistency without cadence. No alignment without ritual.

Because here’s the truth: every scaling company eventually learns that its team doesn’t follow its goals. Your team follows your rhythms.

The Team That Thought They Communicated Well

Last year, I worked with a team that genuinely believed they were excellent communicators.

Everyone was responsive. Everyone felt informed. Everyone “checked in” frequently.

Except for the week before I arrived, they’d had three different conversations about the same client issue – in Teams, in email, and in a hallway.

No one realized they were solving the same problem until the client asked why they’d gotten conflicting answers.

So I asked them basic questions:

How do decisions flow through the team? When do priorities reset? How do cross-functional updates happen? What’s your weekly rhythm?

I got seven different answers.

They didn’t have a communication system. They had communication hope. And hope is not an operating rhythm.

What Life Without Rhythms Actually Feels Like

Without rhythms, every week feels like you’re starting from scratch.

Every Monday is a negotiation. Every meeting is a surprise. Every deadline sneaks up on you. Every question routes back to the founder.

It’s not chaos – it’s just exhausting.

The founder carries what the system should hold. And carrying isn’t scaling.

The Intervention: Three Simple Rhythms

We built three rhythms for that team:

  1. Monday at 9 am: Priority Reset Not a status update. A forcing function. “Here are the three outcomes we’re driving this week. What’s blocking you?”

  2. Wednesday at 2 pm: Cross-Team Sync Not a meeting to share feelings. A coordination ritual. “What decisions do you need from another team to keep moving?”

  3. Friday at 4 pm: Weekly Retro Not a debrief. A feedback loop. “What worked? What didn’t? What do we adjust next week?”

That’s it. Three hours a week.

What Changed

Three weeks later, the founder sent me a message:

“We had our first week where no one interrupted me with a ‘quick question’ that could’ve waited for stand-up.”

Translation: The rhythm was finally doing the work he used to do.

Six months later, he told me:

“I used to spend Monday mornings telling people what to work on. Now I spend them asking what they’re learning.”

Same workload. Same team. Different system.

The rhythm was holding what he used to carry.

Why Rhythms Work When Goals Don’t

Goals are abstractions. Rhythms are behaviors.

Goals say: “We want better accountability.” Rhythms say: “Every Thursday at 2pm, we review commitments.”

Goals motivate. Rhythms deliver.

This is why your resolution isn’t about designing goals for 2026. It’s about designing the loops that make those goals unavoidable.

The Psychology: Rituals Remove Decision Fatigue

Humans are notoriously inconsistent when relying on willpower. But we’re extremely consistent when following rituals.

Rituals remove:

  • Decision fatigue

  • Ambiguity

  • Daily negotiation

  • Emotional bandwidth drain

They create:

  • Predictability

  • Accountability

  • Cohesion

  • Stability

Predictable loops create predictable output.

This is why high-performing teams feel calm even under pressure. Their rituals do the heavy lifting.

The Four Questions Every Rhythm Should Answer

Here’s the test:

Ask four people on your team these questions separately:

1. What are we doing? (Weekly priorities) 2. How are we doing it? (Workflows + handoffs) 3. How is it going? (Progress check) 4. What needs to adjust? (Course correction)

If you get four different answers, you don’t have operating rhythms. You have operating improvisation.

Three Types of Rhythms You Need

1. Forcing Functions (Prevent drift) Monday priority reset that aligns the team before work scatters

2. Feedback Loops (Capture learning) Friday retro that documents what worked and what didn’t

3. Coordination Rituals (Prevent siloing) Wednesday sync that surfaces cross-team dependencies early

Most teams have too many meetings and too few rhythms.

The difference? Meetings are reactive. Rhythms are structural.

“Without Me” Is the Real Test

Your 2026 resolution isn’t: “I will build operating rhythms.”

It’s: “I will build operating rhythms that run without me.”

Because if your system only works when you’re personally enforcing it, you don’t have a system – you have supervision.

Systems should:

  • Run without being pushed

  • Alert you when something is off

  • Maintain their shape during stress

  • Support the team when you’re not in the room

That’s what real scaling feels like. A shift from force to flow.

Where This All Connects

This is where everything from the previous six resolutions comes together:

You’ve audited your system (Resolution #1).

You’ve defined success (Resolution #2).

You’ve clarified decisions (Resolution #3).

You’ve built the bench (Resolution #4).

You’ve mapped bottlenecks (Resolution #5).

You’ve strengthened safety (Resolution #6).

Now you need the rhythms that make all of it sustainable.

Without rhythms, every insight becomes a one-time intervention. With rhythms, every insight becomes a permanent upgrade.

The “After” Picture

When operating rhythms click into place, leaders notice:

  • Fewer surprises

  • Fewer dropped balls

  • Fewer urgent clarifications

  • Shorter meetings

  • Clearer ownership

  • A calmer emotional tone

  • More strategic headspace

Not because people changed. Not because workloads decreased. But because the system began holding what the leader used to carry.

Rhythms create organizational oxygen – the space between the urgent and the important.

You can breathe. Your team can breathe. The work becomes sustainable.

Your Identity Shift for 2026

This resolution isn’t about becoming a more “organized” leader.

It’s about becoming someone who builds rhythms that hold the work – so you don’t have to.

It’s the difference between walking into Monday thinking “What fires need putting out?” versus “What’s on the priority reset this morning?”

Same Monday. Different operating system.

That identity creates structure. Structure creates rhythm. Rhythm creates sustainability.

Because once you see yourself as someone who designs rhythms instead of managing urgency, everything downstream becomes cleaner, lighter, and more predictable.

Your team stops negotiating priorities every morning. Your calendar stops filling with “quick syncs.” Your nervous system stops treating every week like improvisation.

And your business stops depending on your constant intervention.

The Question That Changes Everything

Here’s what I want you to ask yourself:

Are you building a business that runs on your constant intervention, or one that runs on reliable rhythms?

Because intervention is heroic. Rhythms are scalable.

Take 30 seconds: What’s one weekly rhythm you could add that would eliminate a recurring problem? Drop it in the comments – I read every one.

Your 2026 Resolution

Say it out loud: “I will build rhythms that hold the work – so I don’t have to.”

This resolution sticks because identity drives behavior. Not goals. Not intentions. Identity.

Once you see yourself as someone who builds rhythms, your decisions start aligning with that reality automatically.

Not in March. Not after launch. Not once the dust settles. Now.

Because the rhythm you build today is the performance you experience tomorrow.
