Trust vs Power – Rethinking Stakeholder Mapping

Who Are You Really Working With?

Matt Goode21 June 20252 min read Get new pieces by email

# 6C

Trust and power rarely show up in equal measure.

But in any team, org, or project, both shape your success.

This week's tool rethinks classic stakeholder mapping by combining two essential questions:

  • Who has influence here?

  • Who do I actually trust?

Borrowing from Simon Sinek’s story about how SEAL Team 6 selects teammates (they prefer high-trust over high-performance), this map helps you move beyond org charts and into relational clarity.

Watch Simon Sinek's Video

This Week’s Coaching Prompt

“Who has power on this project – and who do I trust to use it well?”

When to Use It

One engineering manager I worked with was struggling to get traction on a high-stakes cross-functional initiative. Her instinct was to align with the loudest exec sponsor. However, when she mapped influence and trust, she realized that the quietest stakeholder – someone junior – had the trust of both frontline staff and leadership. That person became her secret advantage.

Use this map when:

  • The power dynamics are fuzzy

  • You’re navigating politics, silos, or resistance

  • You need to build alignment across differences

The SEAL Grid (Trust vs Influence)

Build a simple 2x2 matrix:

X-Axis: Level of influence
Y-Axis: Level of trust

Now, place your stakeholders into one of four quadrants:

Type

Influence ↓ / Trust →

What to Do

Allies (High Influence, High Trust)

Top-right

Partner deeply. Keep close. Co-create.

Liabilities (High Influence, Low Trust)

Top-left

Proceed with care. Be transparent, document decisions.

Champions (Low Influence, High Trust)

Bottom-right

Empower and elevate. They build culture quietly.

Distractions (Low Influence, Low Trust)

Bottom-left

Don’t over-invest. Stay informed, not in the lead.

3 Reflection Prompts to Try

  • Who has formal power, but little relational trust?

  • Who has relational capital, even if they lack ta itle?

  • Where am I assuming alignment instead of confirming it?

Try It This Week

  • Sketch your grid. Write 2–3 names in each box. Then ask:

  • “What’s one thing I could shift to engage better or protect this group?”

  • Forward this to a teammate in a cross-functional role or a new project lead – it’s one of those tools that unlocks insight instantly.
