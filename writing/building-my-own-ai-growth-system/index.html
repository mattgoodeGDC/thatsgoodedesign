Building My Own AI Growth System: DALE and the Unexpected Consultant

Matt Goode17 October 20257 min read Get new pieces by email

# AI

When I left USAID after the RIF, I didn’t just lose a job – I lost the structure that organized my work and life.

For twenty years as a teacher (heck, I had to use the “facilities” on someone else’s schedule), I’d lived inside other people’s systems. Meetings, bells, deadlines, accountability – they gave shape to my days. Suddenly, I had to build all that from scratch – for myself.

And that loss of structure didn’t just affect my calendar; it seeped into everything – my health, my energy, even my workouts. I stopped taking the focused 60-minute “lunch break” I’d always used for exercise. The cortisol, the stress, the uncertainty all added up. Regrettably, I think I’m the heaviest I’ve ever been.

And because I’m a systems thinker, my response to chaos is always the same: design clarity.

So I did what I’ve always done when I hit a wall – I started mapping.

I sketched what my business needed to function: how leads come in, how relationships progress, and how I wanted every touchpoint to feel human, not transactional. I had the architecture in my head long before I had a tool to match it.

That was the moment DALE began – not as code, but as a coping mechanism.

Meet DALE – My AI Growth Agent

DALE is a fully integrated AI agent that lives inside Google Sheets.

When I get a new LinkedIn follower, Substack subscriber, quiz participant, or have a meaningful conversation, DALE takes over:

  • Reads: the contact details I’ve added – name, role, company, and a short bio, or note from our exchange

  • Classifies: the lead using my proprietary persona map

  • Generates: bullet points for a first-contact or follow-up message

  • Schedules: the next step is to directly post an event to my Google Calendar

And just to be clear: following me on LinkedIn doesn’t drop you into some mechanical selling machine. DALE isn’t a black hole. It’s more like a relational compass.

If you follow, subscribe, or comment – awesome. Chances are, we already know each other, and you’re not exactly my ICP (unless you are, in which case… reach out, lol).

The Unexpected Consultant

My business is, by design, high-touch and relational. That’s the Relator in me – one of my top CliftonStrengths. Relators are driven by genuine connection. We’d rather go deep than wide; we build trust through authenticity, shared effort, and follow-through. It’s not networking for its own sake – it’s about earning the right to collaborate. I genuinely enjoy the people side of the work.

So I built systems for myself so I could spend more time connecting with humans, not marketing or selling.

And now that I’m working for myself, I want to actually enjoy it – not grind endlessly or replace one kind of burnout with another. I want a business that lets me do what I love – coaching, designing clarity, building leaders – while also making space for life. The same principles I teach about sustainable systems apply to me too: structure creates freedom.

From Strengths to Systems

But if I’m being honest, marketing was my Achilles’ heel. Before the RIF, I knew nothing about it. I suck at sales. I’ve got no game, so to speak. I’m not the “pitch” guy – I’m the “listen, understand, and build something that works” guy.

But behind that warmth sits a lot of Strategic and Learner energy – two of my CliftonStrengths that constantly work in tandem. Strategic sees patterns others miss, scanning for the most efficient or elegant path forward. Learner fuels the curiosity that gets me up early, keeps me up late, (and on the laptop when you get home, sorry Gwen), figuring out how things work – and how they could work better next time.

Together, those three strengths – Relator, Strategic, and Learner – are the blueprint behind DALE itself. It’s a system built from the same wiring I bring to coaching and teaching: grounded in connection, driven by curiosity, and guided by pattern recognition. In other words, I didn’t just build DALE to grow my business – I built it to think the way I do.

Every automation, every piece of code, every system I’ve built has been self-taught – the product of long nights and curiosity. My second chapter – serving my country – was cut short by politics. So I had to leap into a third chapter faster than expected.

Instead of trying to become a salesman, I built a system that could translate what I already do best – relational trust, pattern recognition, and strategic structure – into a marketing engine that still feels like me.

That’s the quiet irony of DALE. I built an accountability system for my business… but it’s also an accountability system for me.

And yes, the name “DALE” is a nod to Dale, a business mentor I connected with on LinkedIn early in this process.

A Peek Behind the Curtain

Here’s a small glimpse inside the engine room:

When I click “Run DALE” on a row in my CRM (I programmed a button in Google Sheets, naturally), here’s what happens behind the scenes:

  1. DALE looks at the contact I’ve selected – their name, company, role, and bio – and pulls it all into a structured bundle of data.

  2. It sends that bundle through OpenAI, where my custom system prompt (the “DALE brain”) analyzes it.

  3. In plain English: DALE reads the row, thinks about who this person is, and fills in everything I didn’t already know – their persona type, tone profile, motivators, objections, even what kind of message I should send next.

  4. A few seconds later, the sheet updates itself. Fields that used to be blank now tell me why someone might be stuck, what drives them, and how to start the next conversation.

It’s not just automation – it’s actually Goode Design, letting me do what I’m really good at: Goode Designing.

By automating what I’m not good at – tracking, scheduling, and follow-up discipline – I built in the accountability I needed. Every action DALE generates is added directly to my Google Calendar as an event, with a complete set of instructions and observations in the description. All of it is done automatically.

In other words, the system reminds me to do the things future-me will thank past-me for.

From Bottleneck to Blueprint

Today, every lead, every follow-up, every message I send is part of a coherent operating rhythm. I’ve gone from reaction to intention – from managing chaos to designing flow.

And that’s what I help my clients do, too. Whether it’s leadership systems, decision frameworks, or organizational design, the goal is the same: build clarity that scales.

So yes – DALE helps me grow my business. But more importantly, it keeps me practicing what I preach. I would hardly be able to call myself a systems expert if I couldn’t even apply it to my own practice.

Why Build It Myself?

Because I could – or more precisely, to see if I could. No tool on the market understood the nuance I needed. I wanted my voice to stay consistent – not templated. Off-the-shelf CRMs and AI sales tools couldn’t capture what mattered most: voice, intent, and trust.

But this wasn’t my first time building from scratch.

At USAID, I once built and coded a complete project-management tool – dashboards, metrics, all the bells and whistles you’d expect from an enterprise platform. Our team had been asking for something like that for ages, but for whatever reason – whether the sensitive nature of our work or (more likely) bureaucratic inertia – approvals for commercial tools like Smartsheet never came.

DOGE doesn’t always understand that in government, inefficiency isn’t a bug; sometimes, it’s a feature. But for me, that gap in our tech stack was maddening. So I built the tool myself.

That experience taught me two things:

  1. Constraints can be catalysts.

  2. If the system doesn’t exist, design it.

DALE grew out of that same mindset – a personal need for clarity and accountability – and became a functioning system that actually makes my work easier.

Designing for What’s Next

For the next phase, I’m integrating advanced sales tools – including those built right into LinkedIn – to complement DALE.

I’m also running a handful of small experiments – testing, tracking, collecting data – and baking what I learn back into the next version. Every iteration makes the system a little smarter, a little more human. It’s part of the same cycle I coach my clients through: analyze, refine, redesign.

DALE is growing right alongside me.

And – dear reader – full disclosure: I’m experimenting on you, too.

I collect engagement data from posts like this one. Not in a creepy way, but in a “scientist observing their own behavior” kind of way. What resonates? What falls flat? How do people actually engage with systems thinking in real life? Those insights feed directly into the next generation of DALE – and the next evolution of my business.

Because in the end, the experiment isn’t about automation – it’s about awareness.

The goal isn’t to automate relationships. It’s to refine discovery.

With DALE’s comprehensive persona analysis (think Boolean logic meets behavioral design), when I go fishing, I’ll use the right bait for the right connection.

I’m in the solutions business – and that means finding the people whose problems align precisely with what I’m built to solve.

And you could say, I’m my first client.
