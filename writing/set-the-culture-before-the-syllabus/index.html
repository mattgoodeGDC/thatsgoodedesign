Set the Culture Before the Syllabus

For new school leaders and educators prepping for Day 1

Matt Goode16 August 20251 min read Get new pieces by email

# Schools# CREATE

Here’s the trap:
It’s the week before school starts. You’re tweaking your syllabus, finalizing staff meeting slides, or perhaps, like I used to, the week before, labeling the Chromebooks… and quietly forgetting that culture isn’t just one more thing on your checklist.

It is the checklist.

Whether you’re leading a classroom, a grade team, or a school, the first few weeks will quietly teach people what to expect – what gets rewarded, what gets ignored, and whether this is a place where they feel safe showing up fully.

This Week’s Leadership Prompt:

“What do I want this space to feel like by Week 3 – and what am I signaling on Day 1?”

  • If you’re a principal, what will new staff feel walking into their first meeting with you?

  • If you’re a teacher, what cues are your students picking up before you even start teaching?

  • If you’re a coach or AP, what small moments show you’re listening before leading?

Tool: The Start-of-Year Culture Builder

A 3-step reflection based on the Culture Pulse and Stakeholder Map tools:

  1. One Relationship to Invest In Early
    ➤ Who’s new, quiet, or skeptical – and how will you build trust fast?

  2. One Unspoken Norm to Name Aloud
    ➤ What’s something “everyone knows” but no one says? Make it visible.

  3. One Moment to Invite Voice
    ➤ What’s one low-stakes way you’ll invite others to shape the space?

Set a timer for 10 minutes. Jot quick answers. Then test one idea this week – with your team or students.

Your first week sets the tone more than your first lesson. Start with how you want to lead – not just what you plan to cover.
