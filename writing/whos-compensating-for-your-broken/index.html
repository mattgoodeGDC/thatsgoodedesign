Who's compensating for your broken systems?

Resolution #5: Your Bottleneck Isn’t Incompetence – It’s Invisible Heroics

Matt Goode15 December 20255 min read Get new pieces by email

# Everything runs through me

“Heroics look like help. But they function like bottlenecks.”

This summer, I stood at a whiteboard with a leadership team, mapping their client onboarding process.

On paper, it was seven steps. Clean. Linear. Professional.

Then I asked them to map the real process – not the one in the slide deck.

Who actually touches it? Who checks it? Who corrects it? Where does it stall? Where do people wait? Who becomes the “go-to” when something falls through the cracks?

By the time we finished, the board had 32 steps, five loops, and three unnamed responsibilities that had been silently absorbed by people who didn’t technically own them.

32 steps, yup.

This is Resolution #5 in my series on building a business that holds its shape. [Previous: Stop Absorbing the Shock – Start Building the Bench]

One team member stared at the board and laughed – that uncomfortable laugh when you realize you’ve been working twice as hard as necessary.

“I had no idea I was doing all that.”

Another said: “I didn’t know anyone else was doing any of this.”

The room went quiet.

Because suddenly they could see it: the invisible architecture that had been quietly exhausting them.

And the founder said what I hear in almost every workshop:

“We built a process without realizing how much of it still lives in people’s heads.”

The Hero Who Became the Bottleneck

A few weeks before that workshop, I’d been on a call with another founder.

He told me, almost apologetically: “The team comes to me because I fix things fast.”

He wasn’t bragging. He was exhausted.

So I asked him a simple question: “Is that speed solving the problem, or hiding it?”

He went quiet.

Then: “Oh. I think I’m the bottleneck.”

And he was – but not because he was slow. Because everyone relied on his speed.

He had become the system’s accidental hero – the person everyone routed work through because it was easier than fixing the friction underneath.

Two weeks later, he called me back.

“I stopped answering quick questions on Slack. Forced people to figure it out or bring it to the weekly meeting.”

“And?”

“Three of them figured it out. One brought it to the meeting and we documented the answer. Nobody’s asked me since.”

That’s the hidden truth in most scaling companies: Heroics look like help. But they function like bottlenecks.

Bottlenecks Aren’t People – They’re Patterns

When a company hits complexity, bottlenecks hide inside:

Invisible Handoffs

Sales thinks ops owns the kickoff call. Ops thinks sales does. The client waits three days.

Undefined Ownership

Everyone assumes someone else is tracking the contract details. Nobody is.

Well-Intended “I’ll Just Do It Myself” Moments

The founder answers a question in 30 seconds instead of documenting it. Now that answer lives only in his head.

These aren’t character flaws. These are system blind spots.

And here’s the problem: Nobody can fix what nobody can name.

What Happened After They Could See It

Three weeks after that whiteboard session, the founder texted me a screenshot.

It was a client onboarding that had just completed – in 4 days instead of 11.

“What changed?” I asked.

“We assigned the three orphaned tasks. Gave someone actual ownership of the kickoff call. They just... did it.”

Same people. Same capability. Different system.

That’s the effect of making the invisible visible:

  • Fewer escalations

  • Cleaner ownership

  • Smoother handoffs

  • Less rework

  • More trust in the system

And the founder? Back to strategic altitude.

Not because he worked less – but because he stopped doing the wrong things.

The Hidden Tax of Bottlenecks

Before they mapped it, the team thought the problem was that people were working too slowly.

Turns out, people were working heroically – just inside a system designed to waste their effort.

One of the managers told me later:

“I thought I was bad at my job because I was always behind. Turns out I was doing three people’s jobs – I just didn’t know which parts were actually mine.”

That’s the hidden tax of bottlenecks:

Not just slow work. Not just wasted time. But good people quietly burning out while compensating for bad design.

The Bottleneck-to-Flywheel Shift

In my forthcoming book Clutch Leadership, I call this the Bottleneck-to-Flywheel shift:

Moving from an organization that depends on heroic compensation to an organization that runs on intentional rhythm.

A bottleneck is simply a place where the system still leans too heavily on one person’s knowledge, context, memory, or speed.

A flywheel is when the work moves cleanly – even when that person isn’t there.

The difference? Visibility.

When you illuminate a friction point:

  • Shame drops

  • Clarity rises

  • Collaboration increases

  • The system becomes self-correcting

Humans improve what they can see. Systems fail where the patterns are invisible.

Your Identity Shift for 2026

This resolution isn’t about becoming a better “problem solver.”

It’s about becoming someone who makes friction visible so the system can solve itself.

It’s the difference between walking into a meeting thinking “I need to fix this” versus “Where is the system breaking down?”

Same problem. Different operating system.

That identity creates structure. Structure creates clarity. Clarity creates rhythm.

Because once you see yourself as someone who illuminates system failures instead of compensating for them, everything downstream becomes cleaner, lighter, and more sustainable.

Your team stops needing you to rescue them. Your processes stop depending on heroics. Your organization stops running on invisible labor.

And you stop burning out trying to be everywhere at once.

The Question That Changes Everything

Here’s what I want you to ask yourself:

How many of your “rock stars” are actually just covering for system failures you haven’t mapped yet?

Because heroics feel productive in the moment. But over time, they’re just expensive workarounds for problems you could’ve fixed.

This is where your system audit from Resolution #1 becomes surgical: You’re not just seeing that there’s drag – you’re identifying exactly where it lives.

Take 30 seconds: What’s one task you keep doing yourself because “it’s just faster”? Drop it in the comments – I read every one.

Your 2026 Resolution

Say it out loud: “I will stop compensating with heroics and start designing systems that hold without me.”

This resolution sticks because identity drives behavior. Not goals. Not intentions. Identity.

Once you see yourself as someone who illuminates friction rather than compensating for it, your decisions automatically align with that reality.

Not when the workload spikes. Not when the fires pop. Not when the system cracks. Early. On purpose. With visibility.
