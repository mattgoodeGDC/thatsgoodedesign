You Took a Week Off - But Still Checked Email Every Night

Matt Goode18 October 20253 min read Get new pieces by email

# Everything runs through me

A Personal Reflection

I’m looking ahead at my calendar – and in a couple of weeks, I’m headed to Palm Springs for a break with friends. It’s been on the calendar since before the RIF. I love the desert, and am so looking forward to some night photography in Joshua Tree.

And honestly, it got me thinking.

When we take family vacations, my wife almost always brings her work laptop with her.
Not because she wants to – but because there are things in her role that only she can do.

It’s not about distrust. It’s about structure.
There’s no real backup. No system to hold things up when she’s out.

And it becomes a quiet handbrake on her actually getting away.
The laptop sits in the corner like a little pressure valve – just in case.

That same pattern shows up for so many leaders I coach:
The PTO that’s never truly “off.”
The inbox is tethered to identity.
The break that still includes “just checking real quick…”

That’s not leadership.
That’s load-bearing you.

One Clear Idea

Taking time off is a leadership test – and most leaders are quietly failing it.
Not because they didn’t plan.

But because they didn’t build a system that can breathe without them.

A founder recently told me:

“I was supposed to unplug. But I still checked email at midnight – just to make sure nothing burned down.”

Sound familiar?

What’s breaking isn’t your calendar.
It’s your safety net.

The problem isn’t taking time off.
It’s about tethering yourself to the inbox just to feel safe.

That’s not rest.
That’s a system dependent on you.

And it means agility is missing – not from your product, but from your people.
Because if you can’t step away, your company can’t step up.

This Week’s Coaching Prompt

What needs to change so your absence doesn’t equal anxiety?

Start with one of these:

  • Who assumes the lead when you’re gone – and do they know it?

  • What’s a decision-making gap that keeps you glued to your inbox?

  • Where have you delegated tasks, but not trust?

The CREATE Lens: Agility

This is about resilience – not just individual, but organizational.
Agile companies aren’t just fast. They’re anti-fragile.
And that starts with a leadership system that survives your silence.

If your company’s adaptability hinges on your availability,
then the next sprint might run straight through your vacation.

A Tool to Try: The “Week Off Without Panic” Protocol

Take 15 quiet minutes to map this:

  • Roles – Who owns what while you’re out? Are they empowered to act?

  • Decisions – What’s the threshold for someone needing your input?

  • Emergencies – What counts as one? Who decides that?

  • Comms Norms – What’s the default? Silence? Updates? Daily check-ins?

Then ask a trusted team member:
Would this actually hold if I stepped away tomorrow?

Why This Matters Right Now

Your absence is inevitable – either by design or by disaster.

The founder who never truly leaves never truly leads.
You’re still the glue. But your business needs a guide.

If rest requires constant inbox checks,
your company isn’t agile. It’s fragile.

Your Move: Test a Resilient Week-Off Plan

Run a mini-simulation:

  • What would break?

  • Who would stall?

  • Where do you need to give away more trust?

Don’t wait for a health scare or burnout to force the handoff.
Build it now – so you can leave and exhale.

Please forward this to someone who’s still checking email on vacation –
not because they don’t trust their team,
but because the system makes them feel like they can’t step away.

You don’t have to do more.
You have to build what lets you step back –
so your leadership still speaks, even when you’re silent.
