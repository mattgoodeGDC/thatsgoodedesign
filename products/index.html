Why this is different

You are not buying a methodology with a team attached

A firm sells you a partner and staffs the work with people you have not met. These engagements are the other shape. Five differences, all structural, all checkable.

  • The same person, start to finish. Whoever reads your diagnostic is whoever is in the room a year later. There is no associate layer and no handoff after the sale, which is the whole reason the second half of an engagement is usually better than the first.
  • It starts from instruments, not interviews. CliftonStrengths® and EQ-i 2.0, read against each other rather than reported side by side. Most engagements open with a fortnight of listening and a hypothesis. This one opens with data about how the people in the room actually operate under pressure.
  • COACHING and CONSULTING in one engagement, named. A coach will not tell you what to do. A consultant will not stop telling you. Both modes are in scope here, you always know which one is running, and you can ask to switch mid-conversation.
  • The individual work stays private even when the company pays. Sponsors hear that an engagement is active and nothing else without written consent. That is not a courtesy; it is the condition that makes the one-to-one work honest enough to be worth buying.
  • Scope gets renegotiated at the midpoint, by me. Halfway through, I open the agreement and ask what has changed. A firm has no incentive to raise that question and every incentive to deliver what was sold.

Build or buy

The person who can do the job is probably already in the building

Every leadership gap is answered one of two ways. You develop somebody who already knows how the place works, or you pay a premium for somebody who does not. The second one is more expensive than it looks, and the research on that is not close.

  • 18 to 20% more What external hires are paid, compared with people promoted into the same job from inside.
  • Two years behind External hires post significantly lower performance reviews for their first two years, and spend most of that time learning how to get anything done in a place they do not know yet.
  • More likely to go Higher exit rates, and a materially greater chance of being let go inside those first few years. You pay the premium twice.
  • The bench is the asset The question is not whether your leaders are any good. It is how many deep you are at each position, and what happens the week one of them resigns.

Figures from Matthew Bidwell, Wharton School, “Paying More to Get Less: The Effects of External Hiring versus Internal Mobility,” Administrative Science Quarterly. Not my research, and worth reading before your next search committee.

    One leaderSix sessions, ongoing

    Six structured sessions built on OBD data, so session one starts from a read rather than an introduction. Leadership presence, delegation, team dynamics, decision-making, or whatever the diagnostic actually surfaced rather than whatever was on the brochure. Renewable on completion.

    Two modes, and you always know which one you are in. COACHING is client-led: you set the agenda, and the answer is yours to find. CONSULTING is expert-led: I bring the pattern, the framework and a direct recommendation. Leadership identity, presence, and the decision you already know but will not say out loud are COACHING work. Org design, sequencing, and structural bottlenecks are CONSULTING work. You can ask to switch at any point.

    Most of the real work happens at the seam between the two. A leader who cannot execute a strategy you both agree is right is a CONSULTING diagnosis with a COACHING unlock. A team that keeps breaking down looks operational and is usually about identity. When that happens we name it and choose, rather than pretending the engagement only does one of the two.

    Every session runs the same arc. You open by naming what you want out of the hour, and that becomes the agenda. We follow that thread. We close on what you are committing to before we next meet, which means accountability is built in rather than added on. About a tenth of the value lands in the room; the rest shows up in the decisions you make in the three weeks afterward.

    What is said in a session stays in it. Where an employer, a board or a school is paying, I confirm that the engagement is active and nothing further without your written consent. If a sponsor tries to end it early, you hear that from me directly and can continue privately. The engagement runs to the ICF Code of Ethics, and we re-contract at the midpoint rather than assuming the brief you started with still holds.

    Requires a completed OBD, because six sessions spent working out who you are is five sessions wasted.

The front door

The On-Board Diagnostic

The name is a joke I am going to explain, because a joke nobody gets is just a name. The book is called CLUTCH. The whole argument runs on one extended metaphor: an organization is a drivetrain, leadership is the mechanism that lets its parts turn at different speeds without shearing, and the frameworks are the engine, the gearbox, the navigation and the handoff. So the diagnostic is the OBD, the port under the dashboard a mechanic plugs into before touching anything.

Most assessments tell a leader who they are. The OBD tells them where the engine is already knocking. It is an individual instrument: one leader, one read, one debrief.

It uses two validated instruments, CliftonStrengths® and the EQ-i 2.0. You receive both reports in full, exactly as their publishers issue them, unaltered. What I add is a third document in my own words: a written read of what the two results together suggest about how you operate under pressure, using CREATE and VOICES as the lens. Neither instrument makes that argument on its own, and neither publisher makes it for you.

Participants own their results. The report is designed to be useful by itself, portable to any coach or supervisor, and valid as a personal reference whether or not anything else follows. There is no obligation to continue.

What’s in it

  • Your CliftonStrengths® report, unaltered
  • Your EQ-i 2.0 report, unaltered
  • A guide to reading each one
  • The OBD read, written by me
  • 45-minute 1:1 debrief

Report within 10 business days of assessment completion. Debrief scheduled on delivery. Assessment codes are bought on your behalf and the results are yours to keep, whether or not anything else follows.
